# rl-engine overview

What exists in the engine, by tier and crate, and what does not yet.
This page is kept current: every slice that adds or removes a system updates it in the same commit.
`docs/PLAN.md` holds the reasoning and the milestone history; this page holds only the inventory.

`git log -1 --format=%cs docs/OVERVIEW.md` says when it was last touched; there is no stamp here to drift from it.
`scripts/check-overview.sh` fails the build when a plugin, a design doc or an example is missing below, so the inventory cannot quietly fall behind the code.

## The shape

Four tiers.
Tiers 0 and 1 never depend on Bevy and build for wasm; CI enforces both.
Tier 2 is the Bevy layer that runs the loops.
Tier 3 is the facade a game depends on.
Content is never named in the engine: tiles, damage kinds, stats, statuses, factions, slots, tags, facts, quests and bands are all opaque ids in registries the game fills, and behaviour is taken as traits and closures.

## Tier 0: rl-core

- Geometry: `Point`, `Rect`, `Direction` and `DirectionSet`, `Grid<T>` behind the `Grid2D` trait, Bresenham lines, discs, squares, cones, Chebyshev and Euclidean distance.
- `DisjointSet`.
- Seeds: `RunSeed`, `SeedDomain`, derived streams, position and pair hashes for seams.
- `DiceRoll`, parsed from `NdS+B` at content load.
- `noun`: a thing's one name said of one or of several, `a pebble` or `5 pebbles`, `throwing knife` to `throwing knives` and `bottle of rum` to `bottles of rum`, `scroll titled ZUM KA` to `scrolls titled ZUM KA` by the word before a lower-case connective, with the common English endings and a short list of the irregular; a name that carries an article or a capital is said as written; `counted_as` says a stuff with no `a` by its `Countability`, `chain mail`, and `owned` says a thing as someone's own, `your sword`.
- `Id<T>` typed dense ids with an interner.
- Quantile and rank helpers.
- `TurnQueue<Id>`: an integer clock scheduler with peek, pop, batch dequeue, entries for saving, and speed-scaled costs.

## Tier 1: no Bevy

### rl-grid

- Tile registry with flag tables, `Terrain` and views, `OpacitySource` and `CostSource` traits. A tile names what it opens and closes into, and how it burns and the tile it leaves, resolved to ids in the tables, which refuse a name nobody registered.
- `TileField<T>`: a value per tile stepped a turn at a time by a rule that reads the field as it stood, allocating nothing in a step, and moved with a window by `reframe`.
- `BitGrid`.
- Symmetric shadowcasting field of view.
- Region flood and labelling.
- A* with scratch buffers.
- Region-bounded `DijkstraMap` with scale and rescan for flee maps; descending it takes only a step the flood itself would take, so a mover is never handed the diagonal past a wall's corner that the move resolver refuses.
- `CostSource::lets_diagonal_past(p)`, the one corner rule both `AStar` and `DijkstraMap` ask of a diagonal step's two squeezed cells: `is_passable(p)` unless a source says otherwise, so a source may price a closed door at the cost of opening it while still letting no diagonal cut past its corner, which is what the move resolver answers too.
- `SpatialGrid`.
- Targeting: own, adjacent, bolt, ball, beam and cone shapes resolved to a footprint against two caller-named blockers, what stops a projectile and what stops a burst, `burst`, the cells a straight line from a centre reaches within a radius without crossing a wall, which a ball bursts with and a thrown thing's trigger too, and `clear_shot`.
- Light: `Light` as an intensity, a landed colour and a waver the renderer alone reads, `Emitter` with a flicker, integer falloff to zero at the rim, screen blending, and a `LightField` cast through the shadowcast in an order-independent way, with a flood for glowing areas and a compose over two layers and an ambient; `LightBand::of`, the one rule that names an intensity dark, dim or lit against a seen threshold and a bright one.
- Criterion benches on realistic maps, lighting at twenty sources included. `rl-bevy` has two, on one player turn as the crowd of awake minds grows and on where a pass and a sight recast go; `rl-ui` one on the frame: a still frame, a frame in which a carried lamp moved, and the same by how many panels are up.

### rl-mapgen

- The pass chain: named passes keying their own RNG stream, enforced phase order, fallible passes, typed outputs, snapshots.
- Passes: fill, border, scatter, scatter by a per-cell density, cellular cave, keep largest region, central start.
- Dungeon passes: rooms, BSP, doors, random start, farthest exit.
  A random start may be kept a distance clear of every piece stamped before it, falling back to the plain pick where nowhere is that clear.
- Prefab stamping from ASCII with a legend and marks, placed at a point, centred, or in a room, in any of eight facings, and one stamp may choose among weighted candidates.
  A turned piece takes its marks with it.
  A room-placed stamp never lands where an earlier stamp in the same chain already is.
  A room-placed piece needs a room with floor on every side of it, a cell wider than the piece all round, so its walls never sit on the room's own edge or over a doorway.
  `StampEachRoom` furnishes every room no stamp took, each with a weighted piece that fits it in a facing the stamp allows, slid off the start and the exit when centred would paint or mark either, leaving a room nothing fits as it was; it reads rooms, so a cave or any map whose builder emits none gets nothing from it.
  `Prefab::parse_cells` builds a piece from a `Cell` per glyph, a tile, a mark with the ground to paint under it, or the map left as it was.
  A piece `keyed` by whoever parsed it carries that key through the stamp as `Stamped::prefab`, so each of its marks says which piece it came from; an unkeyed piece stamps exactly as before.
- A place grown by accretion: `Accrete` lays a first piece and attaches every later one to a wall of what already stands, so the place is walkable end to end by construction and its rooms share walls.
  A `Shape` draws a `Piece`, its floor and the sites where it may be joined; the engine ships `Rectangle`, `Cross` and `Passage`, and a game writes its own.
  A `District` is a region's weighted taste in shapes, scattered by density, so one place has regions that differ with no wall between them.
  A `Join` is a door, an arch or a mouth several cells wide.
  A passage left with one way in is turned back into what it was carved from, and growth fails if the open share it was asked for is not reached.
  It emits an `Area` per piece, an `Opening` per join, and a `Room` per area whose shape draws rooms, so the stamp passes fill a grown place as they fill any other.
- `OpenLoops` cuts a door through the wall between two areas that touch but are far apart on foot, longest detour first, one door a pair at most.
- `Survey` measures a grown place into a `Surveyed`: per area its openings, whether it is a dead end, a chokepoint or a junction, and its seclusion from the direct route; per place its loops, its open share and the length of that route.
  `Require` fails a place that falls short of the bounds it is given.
- `Chain::run_retrying` runs a chain over a fresh context again from the next attempt when a pass fails; an attempt is an index into every pass's stream, so one seed builds one place however many attempts it took.

### rl-world

- FBM noise over a sample space.
- Elevation with quantile banding and a continuous height field.
- Priority-flood hydrology with lakes and rivers as a share of land.
- Climate: temperature, moisture, distance to water.
- Cell facts, relief, and a classifier hook the game fills.
- Scored site placement with clearances.
- A turn-cost road router with a distance field.
- The world graph and its rules trait.
- Chunks built from a neighbourhood with a seam hash so edges agree, road and river passes routed by A*.
- Per-tile sampling: warped bilinear climate, a clump field, `tile_facts`.
- A headless `world_dump` example.

### rl-rules

One crate, in modules: crate boundaries follow dependency weight, and content, rules, AI, events and tools all weighed the same.

- `content`: `Registry<T>` loaded from RON with validate-on-load, and `BandedTable` with weights, groups, gap detection and a draw among only the rows a predicate keeps, `pick_where`, with `band_where` saying which band it falls back to. `load_spawns` reads a game's spawn file, rows of `(monster, bands: (first, last), weight, group: (fewest, most))` naming the game's own registry, `group` optional, into a `BandedTable` in the order written, every row that could never be drawn refused at once.
- Stats with a modifier accumulator, each modifier tagged with its `Source`: a status by id and instance, a worn item by handle, or one of the game's own, so the systems that fold them never touch each other's.
- The damage pipeline: kinds, resistances, hits with the attacker and credit split, composable stages. A hit offered below zero is nothing, and whoever rolls a blow floors it at zero; what a resistance past a hundred absorbs is the one negative result, and armor and a block leave it alone.
 Mending is not damage: a status mends with `mends`, reported in `TickReport::mends`, and the Bevy layer's `Heal` is its own path to health.
- `names`: `Names`, the registries a content file's names resolve against, borrowed for a load: the engine's by name, any of a game's own with `with("item", &items)`, and a vocabulary interned while the app is built rather than loaded, such as the sounds, with `interned("sound", &names)`. Every loader below reads through it and reports every unknown name in a file at once, saying which registry was missing when one was never given. A game's own definition type writes a named field as a `NameRef<T>` and loads its file with `Names::load`, so it holds ids from then on, with no validation pass, no second lookup at spawn, and no string that could still be wrong; rows that name content without being content, such as a spawn table with a row per band, load the same way with `Names::load_list`, errors named by row; a `NameRef` read any other way refuses to deserialize.
- Statuses with stacking rules, per-turn ticks, mends and cures, loaded by name with `status::load`: a stat to modify, a damage kind to tick or an amount to mend, each resolved to a typed id, `unseen`, a status whose holder nothing sees while it lasts, `boon`, a status good for its holder, which the narrator tells as good news, `holds`, a status whose holder loses its turns while it lasts, which `Statuses::holding` names, and `sleeps`, a status that holds the same way and ends the moment it is woken, by `Statuses::wake`. One put on for `UNTIL_CURED` turns bites and mends every turn and is never counted down, so only a cure ends it: what a body is, or what a worn thing gives for as long as it is worn.
- A faction relation matrix.
- The equipment slot graph with displacement, and `Equipment::equip_keeping`, the same equip pushing off nothing that stays on: a thing that may go in several slots takes the one whose occupant may come off, and with none it is refused as `EquipError::Kept`.
- Authored parts, `rl_rules::parts`: the shape of every field a game's own item or monster definition writes for something the engine already simulates, read through `Names` with everything past the essentials optional and absent meaning no mechanic. `Melee`, `Ranged` and `Throw` are a blow, a shot and a throw; `Wear` is where a thing is worn, `either` reaching for the pair its slot's `SlotDef::pair` names, found by `equip::pair_of`; `Charges` is what a use costs, its refill in hundredths of a step and whether it is `while_worn`; `Level` is what one level adds, `armor`, `damage`, `amount`, `turns`, `pulse`, `charges` and `stats`, between an optional `min` and `max`, applied linearly and nothing else, and `Level::over` writes a line's own over a game's default. `parts::resistances` reads a resist list, a kind written twice resisting by both. A monster's definition types its blow and shot with the same `Melee` and `Ranged`, the span it keeps from its enemies with `KeepAway { within, no_closer }`, and its drops as `(item, percent)` rows that `parts::drops` reads into a `DropTable`. `AttackShape` and `WhenEmpty` live here, re-exported where they were. Item tags are `tag::TagDef`.
- `loot`: where items turn up, keyed by a game's own item ids. `LootTable`, rows of an item, its bands, a weight and a group, sorted by item name so the order a spawn file is written in draws nothing; `pick` by weight and group, and `pick_tagged` among the items carrying a tag, falling back to the nearest band above that has one, so a request past the deepest row gets the deepest thing; `DropTable`, each row rolled on its own with a chance and a count; `ScatterRules` and `plan_scatter`, a count beside each place mark of a tag and a range of loose items more for each band, one to a cell and never on a mark; and `load`, a spawn file resolved against the game's own registry.
- `layout`: a layout file, `LayoutDef`, the shapes a place is grown from, the districts that favor some of them and the recipes a place names, with every name resolved at load and every mistake reported at once; the growing is `rl-mapgen`'s.
- `namegen`: names drawn from syllables. `load` reads a `NameBook`, a file of named `NameSet`s, each pools of syllables named by the set, patterns of `Pool`, `Maybe` and `Text` parts drawn by weight, a `NameCase`, a length and forbidden substrings, refused at load with every mistake named by its set; `NameSet::draw` puts a name together from the caller's stream and draws again while libtcod's rejections refuse it (three of a letter running, a pair repeated back to back, a run of three repeated, a stray space, a forbidden substring, the length) and one of its own, a word given twice; the case is applied to syllables and never to a pattern's text, up to `ATTEMPTS` times and then `None`, and `draw_distinct` also refuses what the caller says is taken. `docs/design/names.md` is the reasoning.
- `prefab`: a prefab file, `PrefabDef`, rows of glyphs and a legend giving each one a tile or a `Slot`: a prop, an item row (the `ContentRoll` a container's contents are), a monster by `Pick::Kind` or `Pick::Role` with a band offset, or a `Mark` left to the game. `load` resolves every name and reports every mistake at once. `coverage` runs every drawn slot against a game's `Sources` at every band, and `Coverage` says, as a `Reach` per band, where a draw lands, falls back or finds nothing.
- `role`: `RoleDef`, a name and the monsters that fit it, loaded by `load` from a map of role to members; `draw` is the game's spawn table restricted to the role's members at a band, through `BandedTable::pick_where`, and `band_for` the band it falls back to, the way a tagged loot draw does.
- `ability`: what an actor can spend a turn on besides a step and a swing, as data. A shape from the targeting module, an [`Aim`] saying what it wants under it, costs against a pool, health or a tagged item, requirements over statuses, slots and stats, an integer time and cooldown, and a list of named effects with their arguments left unparsed for whoever registered them. `load` resolves every name in a file through `Names`; `blocked` answers whether a use is permitted and lists every reason it is not. `Aim::hits` says who a footprint catches, the user counting as its own ally, and `Aim::worth_aiming_at` what is worth pointing it at; `Targeting` gathers a shape, an `Aim` and whether the cell must be seen, which `AbilityDef::targeting` gives for an ability and a thing's aimed use writes for itself, and `aim_blocked` takes one and refuses an aim with nowhere to go or out of the user's sight. `TriggerSpec` beside `EffectSpec` is the authored form of what a prop or an item does at a moment: the moment by name, `Area::Here` or `Burst`, how many times it fires, a list of its own or none, to deliver the thing's shared one, on a `pulse` trigger only the `every` it comes round at, refused on any other, and on a `use` trigger only an `aim`, a `Targeting` for a thing used at somewhere, or an `asks`, the prompt and the optional tag of a thing used on another of its user's things.
- `ai`: movement profiles, snapshots of what an actor sees, and a tactic-priority brain with melee, flee-when-hurt, hunt, wander and use-ability tactics. `Wits` is what a mind is able to do whatever its brain would like, as capabilities (flees, searches, opens doors, picks up, equips, throws, travels, uses what it carries) with mindless, animal and sapient presets, read from content as a preset or a list; the tactics that need one ask the snapshot first. The snapshot also holds what the actor carries to throw, to use and what it sees lying about, with how much better off it would be wearing each; `ThrowAtRange` throws down a clear line at what is out of arm's reach, `ShootAtRange` fires what the mind wields down a clear line at an enemy two or more tiles off, `UseCarried` drinks what mends when hurt and points what is aimed at foes or allies as an ability of that aim would be pointed, and `Scavenge` fetches gear better than what it wears, or something to throw while it has nothing to throw, walking round what is in the way and putting gear on where it lies. `Standard` is the chain most monsters decide with, as a game's choices: which of `FleeWhenHurt`, `Keep::enemies`, `UseAbility`, `MeleeAdjacent`, `ShootAtRange`, `ThrowAtRange`, `Scavenge`, `Hunt`, `SearchLastKnown`, `KeepPost` and `Wander` it holds, in that order or with running after the attacks, and every chance and reach, none of them the engine's; `Standard::brain(repertoire)` builds it for one kind, each step only where its `Repertoire` can take it, and `StandardBrain` takes a game's own tactics `first`, `before_hunt`, `instead_of_hunt` and `before_wander`. `Wits::takes_things` is whether a mind takes anything up, what `Scavenge` fetches for.
`Keep` keeps station on allies, on enemies or, as `Keep::leader`, on the one actor its follower's `Leader` names, through the `Led` sense a follower is told its leader's place by, seen or not, falling back to keeping with its allies while that one is gone. `Explore` steps toward the nearest ground an `Uncharted` sense lists, by the way there, and passes when the list is empty or none of it can be reached. `KeepPost` walks an actor with a `Posted` sense back to that cell with nothing better to do and waits there, and passes for one with none, so it can sit in every brain. The ability tactic aims by `Aim::worth_aiming_at` and scores a footprint by `Aim::hits`, the rules the resolver lands it with, so a mind fires something it cannot understand, never learns the theme, and never counts a hit the resolver would not land.
- `events`: facts with kind, subject, object and amount, matchers, a ledger of named counters that `reset` puts back to nought with its rules kept, and quests as objectives over facts with prerequisite chains and a victory flag.
- `balance`: threat scoring and the spawn-band report.
- `ai::awareness`: `NoticeStats` (a certain radius, a chance beyond it, a light bonus and a memory) and `StealthStats`, the pure `notices` roll, and `Awareness`, which goes `Unaware` to `Alert` on a sighting and back once its memory runs out; plus the `SearchLastKnown` tactic, which walks to where an enemy was last seen.
- `ai::hearing`: `HearingStats` (a threshold a sound must arrive above, and a memory), `carries`, which reads what a sound passes from the flags a tile already has (open ground at a step, a closed door at a step and a muffle more, a wall not at all), and `left_after` and `heard`, in whole steps of loudness spent in hundredths of a step.
- `gas`: `GasDef` (spread, fade, the concentration that hides what is behind it, whether it burns, a status for breathing enough), loaded by status name; `release`, an amount let go at a point that fills the cell to `FULL` and spills the rest to the nearest cells at once, so a grenade's worth is a room of smoke and a plume down a corridor; and `diffuse`, an exchange with each neighbour and a swirl pushed one way per patch, both from `spread`, then held to the densest cell around and faded by at least one unit, so every cloud clears and no two spread alike.
- `fire`: `Tinder` and `spread`, one catch chance per burning neighbour from rolls the caller passes in, so the spread does not depend on visiting order.
- `accuracy`: whether an attack lands. A `HitModel` turns a `Shot` of plain facts (how it travels, distance, effective range and reach, the `LightBand` at the target, accuracy and evasion) into `Odds`, some chances in some outcomes with the labelled `Line`s behind them, rolled with one draw; `Certain` never rolls and `Percent` is accuracy less evasion, less `range_penalty` and a dim or dark penalty, clamped to 0..=100.
- `forecast`: what a fight is likely to cost, with the average roll put through the game's own mitigation pipeline in place of a real one; blows and turns to fell either side, weighed by each side's own blow cost and speed, and an `Outlook` read off the two counts; a `Combatant`'s chance to hit scales what it is expected to deal. `Arms::at` picks what a side fights with at a distance by the resolver's own rule, and `Arms::closing` what it would fight with once in reach, its shot if it has one and its blow if not, so a panel still reads in turns across the room and only a side with no attack at all reads never.
  Pure, so an inspect panel's numbers cannot drift from the fight.

## Tier 2: the Bevy layer

### rl-bevy

Every plugin at a glance, for a reader coming back to it.
"Needs" lists what a plugin cannot work without: the plugins it checks for in `finish`, then, after the semicolon, the resources it declares with `app.needs`, whose absence is reported with how to make one when play begins.
Its opposite is `app.reads::<M>()`, which says a plugin reads a message another plugin writes and that an empty queue is a fine answer: a panel that shows what the player heard works in a game with no noise, rather than panicking because nobody registered the message.
A third declaration, `app.reset_on_new_run::<R>()`, says what a plugin keeps of a run and has the engine put it back when the run ends, so `clear_run` names no subsystem's resource.
`cue::Airborne<L>`, behind `app.add_airborne::<L>()`, is what a resolver puts in the air while its flight is watched; combat, throwing, abilities and the effects a thing's aimed use lands each register one and keep only their own `land`.

| `KeyScriptPlugin` | - | `press`, keys played the way a keyboard does, for a headless test | - |

- One plugin per subsystem, each opt-in: `CorePlugin` holds the loop, the map and its places; field of view, combat, minds, statuses, items, lighting, streaming and facts are added by name. A plugin says what it needs with `app.needs::<R>(plugin, hint)`, and on entering play one check lists every missing piece at once with how to make it, `CorePlugin`'s own `WorldMap` included, rather than a subsystem, or the whole frame, quietly doing nothing all run. A plugin that depends on another checks in `finish`, so the order a game lists its plugins in never matters, and a world built while play never began is warned about.
- The engine-owned loop: a `Turn` schedule run as many passes per frame as it takes, input once per frame, refusals that cost nothing, stall recovery.
- A run nobody plays: a `Player` that is no `Actor` is an onlooker, never admitted to the queue and so never dealt a turn, which the map being read, the view, the sighted list and the narrator follow as they follow any player; `Viewshed::everywhere()` is what it carries to be shown the whole window, cast as both grids set whole; and `Pace`, a core resource, is how fast the turns run while nobody is waited on, `unpaced` by default, `per_second(hundredths)` of game time for each second of the wall clock earned by `earn_pace` and spent by `run_turns` as the clock advances, or `stopped`. A turn already dealt is resolved at any pace, and the pace changes how many passes a frame runs and nothing a pass does. `docs/design/watching.md` is the reasoning.
- Replay: `Recording`, a run as its seed, its arguments and every frame's keys stamped with the turn clock they were read at, written as RON; `replay::args` and `replay::seed`, what a game reads its flags and seed through so a replay runs with the ones it was recorded under, `Seed::from_args` included.
- Cues: what a turn did that is worth seeing, as `Cued` messages the resolvers write, each a `Cue::Flight` between two `Anchor`s or a `Cue::Burst` on some, lit at once or going out `from` one of them, in a `LookOf` the drawer resolves; an ability's burst goes out from where its projectile stopped or else from its user, and a trigger's from where its moment happened. An anchor is a cell or an entity on one, followed as it moves. `TurnHold`: a plugin that plays cues `watch`es, and the loop then stops after any pass that cued something and runs no pass until the plugin lets go, so two casters are seen one after the other and a spit at the player is seen to arrive before it hurts. A flight lands when it has been seen: with something watching, the ability resolver, the throw resolver, the attack resolver and the landing of a thing's aimed use cue the flight, put the landing aside in their `Airborne`, and spend the turn; no turn is dealt while anything is in the air; the first pass after the hold lets go lands it, which is when the burst is cued, the effects run, the blow falls, the shot hits and the knife lies where it fell; and the burst is waited on in turn. Without a watcher a use lands as it is cast. An effect writes its own cues through `EffectWorld::cues`.
- `Seed`, the run's seed, the one thing about randomness a game supplies, with `Seed::from_args` for `--seed`. Each subsystem that rolls owns a `Stream` (`CombatRng`, `EffectRng`) that `add_stream` derives from the seed before the first turn and again whenever it changes, so a continued run reseeds by setting it; `Seed::stream(name, index)` is a game's own named draw, and `EngineSave` records the seed it captures.
- A mind may choose an action the engine has never heard of: a tactic returns `Decision::own(choice)`, any type implementing `Choice`, the engine reports it as `MindChose`, and `add_choice::<A>()` routes a choice that is also an action to its own `Intent<A>` in `DecideSet::Game`, with the sweeper refusing one nobody routed. A game's tactic can sit anywhere in the priority list beside the engine's.
- A reaction phase inside the turn: `TurnSet::React` runs after the actions of a pass resolve and before the turn is requeued, which is where a game answers what just happened. A drink heals before the next blow lands, a bite poisons on the bite, gear counts from the moment it is worn. After it, `TurnSet::Listen` hears the pass's noises and `TurnSet::Record` reads what the pass did for the log, once every reaction is in.
- Drawing is layered by `PresentSet`: narration, then the map, then the chrome, then whatever covers them. No crate orders itself after another crate's draw function.
- Actions are types, not a list: `Step`, `Attack`, `Wait`, `Open`, `Close`, `PickUp`, `DropItem`, `Equip`, `EquipFromGround`, `Unequip`, `UseItem`, `UseItemAt`, `UseOn`, `Throw`, `GoThrough` and `Bump` ship with the engine, each resolved by the module that owns the mechanic. `Bump` is an alternate action: read in `ResolveSet::Redirect`, the stage before any resolver claims a turn, it becomes a `Step`, an `Open` or an `Attack` by what is in the way, and a bump into someone who is not a foe is a free refusal reported as `Bumped`, unless `BumpRules::swap_allies` makes a bump into an ally a `Swap`, the two changing places for the cost of the step, reported as `Swapped`. A game's own alternate follows the same shape. Doors are their own actions, since working one changes what blocks sight; a step into a shut door is refused. A game registers its own with `add_action`, resolves it in `ResolveSet::Act` through `Resolution`, and the sweep refuses whatever no resolver claimed. `Resolution` is every resolver's side of the loop, the engine's and a game's: `claim` the actor holding the turn, then `done` with a cost or `failed`, which keeps the player's turn and charges anyone else, so no resolver has to remember that a monster handed a free retry loops forever.
- Every stage a plugin fills is a named set, and no system orders itself after another's function: `DecideSet::{Light, Sense, Notice, Offer, Perceive, Minds, Game}` with `PerceiveSet::{Begin, Roster, Filter, Annotate}` inside `Perceive`, `ResolveSet::{Redirect, Travel, Act, Triggers, Fields, Effects, Damage}` with `LandSet::{Ability, Throw, Shot}` inside `Act`, so what was in the air lands in one fixed order and a kill's credit never rests on the scheduler, `FieldSet::{Fire, Gas}` inside `Fields` with steps, waits and warps travelling before anything else acts, and `CleanupSet::{Remove, Requeue}` taking the dead out before a turn is requeued. `Last` is `EndOfFrame::{Save, Bury, Restart}`: the run is written down, then the dead and the spent are buried, then a restart or a leaving asked for this frame tears the run down, so no save is written of a world with no run in it.
- The run's life: a game's start system goes in the `NewRun` schedule, run at startup unless a title holds the start and again after every `Restart`, which tears the run down (everything on a map despawned, the clock, the index, the knowledge, the fields and the flow fields reset, the map removed so a forgotten one is reported as before) after running `EndRun`, where a game forgets what it keeps of its own. `RunOver`, written by the game with an `Outcome` and its own epitaph, or by combat on the player's death unless `CombatRules::death_is_not_the_end`, keeps the `Ending` and moves `EngineState` to `Over`, where the world is drawn and nothing in it runs.
- A title's hold on the run, in `title`: `HoldStart`, a resource, keeps the engine from beginning any run at startup while it is present, and `begin_run` takes it away and runs `NewRun`, the one door every run comes through, the engine's own at startup and after a restart included, so no game's start asks whether a title is up. `LeaveRun` tears a run down the way a restart does and holds the start again in place of a new run, with a fresh seed set for the next, winning over a restart asked in the same frame and changing nothing with no run standing. `SavedRun` is what the run's save slot holds, `Empty`, `Readable` or `Unreadable`, inserted and kept by whoever keeps the slot, so a game with none keeps no slot; `ContinueRun` asks a game's `NewRun` systems to restore the saved run, and whichever restores it removes it; `AbandonRun`, triggered on the world, asks whoever keeps the slot to forget it before the trigger returns. Plain words in the crate both the title and the save depend on, so the screen never depends on storage.
- Run records, opt-in by adding `RecordsPlugin`: a game writes `RecordRun { score, summary, sections }` once its run is over, and the engine completes it into a `RunRecord` with the outcome and the turn from the `Ending`, the run's seed and, off wasm, the time it ended, files it into `RunRecords` best score first, a tie keeping the newer ahead and the list a hundred deep at most, and keeps it as `this_run` until the run is torn down, for the end screen and a records screen to read. A record written in the pass that ended the run waits for the `Ending` rather than being lost, and one still waiting when its run is torn down goes with the run, never onto the next run's ending. `docs/design/records.md` is the reasoning.
- Chunk streaming with edit deltas, per-map occupancy and knowledge, field of view.
- `Registries`, every registry the engine reads, in one resource the game fills once: damage kinds, factions, stats, statuses, tags and slots. An empty one means none, and `names()` hands the same tables to a load, so a game's content is resolved against exactly what the engine will read it with and no subsystem keeps a copy.
- Combat: health, armor, resists, factions as `CombatRules`, the matrix over the registry's sides, built by naming pairs with `CombatRules::new(&sides).hostile(a, b)`, `hunts` for a grudge that is not returned and `allied`, melee and ranged attacks down a line of fire, extra strikes, the damage event pipeline, and deaths that linger until the frame ends.
  It knows nothing of minds or abilities: a blow is a blow whoever chose it.
  A weapon states what one blow with it costs, in hundredths of a step; stating nothing costs an ordinary turn.
  `MeleeAttack::new` and `RangedAttack::new` name the kind and the roll, and `costing`, `looking`, `shaped` and a blow's own `unaware` add the rest, so a field only some games want never touches a call site.
  An `AttackShape` strikes more than the target, `Sweep` every foe beside the attacker and `Pierce(n)` the foes in the next `n` cells beyond it in line, each with its own roll, as one swing, one `Struck` and one `fire` moment; a blow on a target that has not noticed its attacker, by stealth's `Watchers::unaware`, lands at the blow's own percent or at `CombatRules::unaware`, and a shot never does.
An actor carrying `Triggers` answers its own `hit` when its blow or shot lands, `struck` when one lands on it, before the damage, and `died` when it dies, through a `Remnant` as a prop's `destroyed` does, crediting what it lands to itself and sparing itself from its own area, unlike a prop's or a thing's.
  An attack's `look` belongs to what attacks, never to its damage kind: a shot with one is cued as a `Cue::Flight` from the shooter to the target, and while something watches its hits wait in `AirborneShots` until `land_shots` lands them on the first pass after the flight has been seen, on a target still standing; a blow with one bursts on its target and hurts at once; an attack with none is cued as nothing and lands as it is made.
- `Loadout`, the one answer to what an actor fights with, summed at the moment of the blow from three layers: its own `Armor`, `Resists`, `MeleeAttack`, `RangedAttack` and `Strikes`, the same components on every item in its `Equipped` slots in slot order, and the stats `CombatRules` names with `armor_stat` and `attack_stat`. A worn blow or shot replaces the actor's own; armor, resistances and extra strikes add up. The attack resolver strikes with it, `apply_damage` defends with it, and the inspect forecast, the vitals strip and the character sheet read it, so nothing is ever copied onto a wearer and a game never rebuilds an actor's attack from its gear.
  Every attack that finds a weapon writes `Struck`, naming the worn item it came from, so a game can heat or spend a weapon without working out which one the loadout chose.
  A worn thing's attack put by as `Stowed`, a weapon gone dry or one a game benched for heat, is not there to find, so `Loadout` falls through to the next worn thing, and `Loadout::dry` says by name whether one is dry.
- Doors: walking into a tile that opens opens it and spends the turn where the actor stands, `Close` shuts one unless something stands or lies in the doorway, and `DoorEvent` reports both, for an actor with the wits to or one with no `Intelligence` at all. `WorldMap::cost_epoch` changes when an edit changes where anyone may walk.
- Minds, opt-in by adding `MindsPlugin`, with or without combat: `Mind`, `Perception` (the radius of the mind's own `Viewshed`, cast the way the player's is and recast in `DecideSet::Sense` when it moved) and `Profile` on a monster, `Intelligence(Wits)`, which every mind carries and is sapient unless the spawn says otherwise, with `Wits::TRAVELS` in no preset for a kind that follows through a way to another map and leaves by one when it runs, and `Trails`, whom such a mind was after or beside on its last turn, and `CameFrom`, where it last stepped from; `Post`, the cell an actor was set to stand on, saved with it and pushed into its snapshot as the `Posted` sense by `sense_posts` in `PerceiveSet::Annotate`; `FlowFields`, one field per set of goal cells, movement profile and whether the mover opens doors (which reads a closed door through `WorldMap::opening_view` as the turn to open it), toward or away, built on demand behind the `Fields` seam a tactic asks with `step_toward` and `step_away_from`, kept until the map's cost epoch changes and bounded, the field asked for longest ago going first, with a field toward up to four goals composed from one flood per goal, so hunters after one player share one flood and two that see different enemies share the flood from each enemy both see, and keeping station is one tactic for every roster it can read, `Keep { on: Station::Allies | Station::Enemies | Station::Leader, keep_within, no_closer_than }`: a companion keeps station on its allies, a spotter or a skirmisher on its enemies, and a follower on the one actor its `Leader(Entity)` component names, through the `Led` sense `sense_leaders` pushes, seen or not, falling back to its allies while that leader is dead, elsewhere or gone; each closes past one distance and backs off inside another, never by a step that undoes what it is doing, in sight when a step allows, and, toward enemies, keeping the same distance from where it last saw one once it cannot see any, with `Hover` beneath it to hold the ground between; and the one system that turns a decision into a step, an attack, a wait, an ability, a pickup, an equip from the ground, a throw or a `MindChose`. What a mind knows is not built there: `Thinking`, a core resource, holds the snapshot while `DecideSet::Perceive` fills it, one contributor per plugin in `PerceiveSet::{Begin, Roster, Filter, Annotate}` order: the roster sorts who is seen into enemies, allies and others by the faction matrix when there is one and into others when there is not (health and side are optional on an `ActorView`, so a civilian is seen and stepped round, with `GiveWay`), stealth takes out the unseen and hiders that have not been noticed, items say what is carried and what lies about, and, for a mind with `Wits::USES`, what it may use as `Snapshot::carried`, combat how far the mind's own shot reaches, worn or built in, abilities what may be used, keeping a self-aimed one only when `Effect::would_act` says its landing would act on something, fire where not to step, and a game pushes a `Sense` of its own for its own tactic to read by type. The snapshot is sorted once, at the head of `Minds`, so the order the contributors ran in never reaches a tactic. The minds roll from `MindRng`, their own stream, and register the `Attack` action themselves, so a game with no combat fields minds that drift and give way and a blow nobody resolves is refused. A `Mind` spawned without the plugin is reported once rather than left standing.
- Charts, part of `MindsPlugin`: what someone other than the player has seen. `Charts` holds a `TileSet` per `ChartId` and map, the id being whatever the game numbers, a scout, a party or a side; a mind carrying `Charting(id)` has what it sees written in by `chart_sight` in `DecideSet::Sense` on each of its turns, and is told by `sense_uncharted` in `PerceiveSet::Annotate`, as the `Uncharted` sense, every cell of the window its chart lacks that could be stood on beside a held one that could, which is what `Explore` walks to. Minds naming one chart share it; `Charts::copy` hands what one holds to another and `Charts::forget` empties one; the resource is emptied when a run ends and saved in `EngineSave`. `TileSet` is the bucketed set of tiles `Knowledge` always kept, now the one type both hold. `docs/design/charts.md` is the reasoning.
- Items on the ground, in bags and in slots, with stacks, tags and enchantments. A thing is named once, singular, and every panel and the narrator say it with its count through `noun`, so one thrown from a stack reads as one and the rest as many. A carried item is on no map, and one put down lies on the map it is put down on. What an item does is its `Triggers`, and `docs/design/items.md` says why an item never lends an ability: a `use` trigger lands on whoever used it where they stand, or, with a `targeting`, at a cell it is pointed at with `UseItemAt`, pointed, previewed and refused by an ability's own rule through `Bystanders::land`, or, with an `asks`, on another of its user's things chosen in the bag and written `UseOn`, which the landing hands its effects as `Landing::on`; `may_use` and `may_use_on` are the rules the resolver refuses a use by, public so the targeting cursor and the bag offer nothing it would refuse; a `land` trigger where it comes down when thrown, and `fire` and `hit` triggers when it is worn as a weapon, a wand being a weapon whose shot carries effects; a trigger with no list of its own delivers the item's shared `effects`, so a draught drunk and a draught thrown are one list. `Consumable` counts what those moments cost it, `left` of `max` charges, a stack spent a unit at a time, then destroyed or kept empty, and an empty thing is neither used nor fired. A use of a thing with a `use` trigger is narrated, named as one of it; an item with no trigger reports `ItemEvent::Used` for the game to answer. A thing spent to nothing is `Spent`: out of every bag, slot and cell at the end of the pass and despawned at the end of the frame, as the dead are, so the log names it in its own colour. Nothing in a bag is in `Known`, so a bag of stims is not a list of abilities. The bag screen describes each trigger in its effects' own words, `use:` and `on landing:`, said once under `use or thrown` when a thing's one of each say the same, with a thing's charges, offers the use key only where it does something, opens the targeting cursor through `AimUse` for an aimed use and turns to choosing for one that asks, and counts the rows it has no room for. `Unknown` keeps a thing's worth off the bag, the look cursor's panel, the targeting box and the gear rail, which say it is unknown, or in the bag and the look cursor what the game says it seems, and marks the vitals' armor uncertain while one is worn, leaving what it does untouched; `Stuck` keeps a worn thing on, refusing taking it off, dropping, throwing and pushing it off with something else, said as `ItemEvent::WouldNotComeOff`, and `equip_keeping` picks the slot whose occupant may come off; both are the game's to put on, take off and save. `EquipFromGround` takes up and puts on what lies underfoot for a turn and a half; `GearScore` is what wearing an item is worth, which a mind compares against what it would displace and, for a thing whose blow would replace its own, against its `OwnBlowScore`; what a dead actor carried falls where it died.
- What wearing an item does is on the item: its combat components, which `Loadout` reads, and `Bestows`, the changes it makes to registered stats, which `fold_gear` puts onto the wearer's `StatBlock` under `Source::Item` whenever its `Equipped` changes and strips again with the item. A wearer spawned already dressed is folded on its first pass, so a restored run rebuilds its gear modifiers from its slots and a save never writes them down. A worn thing may keep its own clock, `Pulse`, reporting the `pulse` moment each period it is worn and restarting each time it goes on, and a worn thing whose charges are `Attuned` refills only while worn and is emptied each time it is put on. A status a worn thing's trigger put on with `while_worn` is held by the thing, and `end_unworn_holds` cures it in the pass the thing is no longer worn, however it came off; a save keeps the holder. `ItemsPlugin` gives every actor a `StatBlock`, as `StatusPlugin` does, so gear stats work in a game without statuses.
- Authored parts made components, `rl_bevy::parts`, for a game that wants them: `Attacks { melee, ranged, throw, cost }` inserts a blow, a shot and a throw at one cost, on a weapon or a monster alike, through `MeleeAttack::from_part`, `RangedAttack::from_part` and `Throwable::from_part`. `ItemParts` is every part of one kind of item, which a game builds once from its own definition, its triggers built and its wear resolved, and `ItemParts::check` names what the engine would never play; `insert_parts(e, parts, level)` inserts `Item`, `MadeOf`, an `ItemLevel` for a thing that takes one, and whichever of `Tagged`, `Triggers`, `Pulse`, `Consumable`, `Attuned`, `Wearable`, `Ammo`, `Armor`, `Resists`, the attacks, `Bestows`, `EffectBonus`, `LightSource` and `Stack` the parts describe, the level held within its part's bounds and applied linearly. `parts::level_up(world, item, by)` moves a thing's level and fits every component the level decides from the parts it was made of, so a level is never applied twice; it touches nothing a game owns, `Stuck` included. `ActorParts<I>` is every part of one kind of actor, every field optional: its `hp`, `armor`, `faction`, `speed`, `perception`, `wits`, `notice`, `hearing`, `dark_sight`, attacks, `light`, `abilities`, the `statuses` it holds for life, `triggers`, `resists`, `drops` of the game's item `I`, `leaves_remains` and the `mind` it decides with, with `flee_at` and `keep_away` for the brain built from them; `ActorParts::insert` puts on a spawned actor the component each written one describes and nothing for one not written, a nought included as written, `ActorParts::over` lays a kind over a game's own defaults, its values winning and the lists added, `ActorParts::check` names what the engine would never play, `ActorParts::repertoire` is what a kind can do for the standard chain, and `held_for_life` is the statuses an actor holds from its first turn, for a game that holds one more of its own. A game adds its own look, name and components beside these.
- Throwing, opt-in by adding `ThrowingPlugin` beside items and combat: `Throwable` says how far an item goes and what it strikes for, `Throw` sends one from a stack or the item itself at a cell, and it strikes the first body in its way down combat's damage pipeline and comes to rest at its feet, short of a wall, or at the end of its reach, reported as `ItemEvent::Thrown`. `flight` is the one answer to where a throw goes, and the resolver and the targeting preview both call it.
- Ammunition, opt-in by adding `AmmoPlugin` beside combat and items: `Ammo { tag }` on a weapon is what one shot spends from its wielder's bag, `spend_ammo` takes it off a stack carrying the tag on every ranged `Struck`, and `sync_ammo` reads every fed weapon's bag every pass, making one with none of its tag left `Dry`, its shot put by as `Stowed`, and loading it again once the bag holds some, writing `AmmoChanged` for each change; the gear rail says `N left` or `dry` as a facet. Foundry's slugs and Undermere's arrows both run on it, and `docs/design/items.md` section 9 is the reasoning.
- Abilities, opt-in, with every actor given an empty `Known`, `Pools` and `Cooldowns` as it is spawned, so `Grants` alone is enough to use what was granted: the `Use` action, `Known` rebuilt every turn from the actor's own `Grants` and nothing it carries, `Pools` for whatever a game calls its fuel, `Cooldowns` as absolute times on the turn clock so a save restores them for nothing, `Offered`, the gate's answer for whoever holds the turn, which the minds and a menu both read through accessors that answer only for that actor, and a resolver that gates, pays, resolves the footprint and lands the effects inside the pass. `Bystanders::land` is the one answer to where a use lands, who it hits and why the aim would be refused; the resolver and the targeting preview both call it. Effects are `EffectsPlugin`'s, and types, not a list: one per subsystem the engine owns, seven in `effects` registered together, gathered into an `Effects`, the one built list, which an ability, a prop's offer and the triggers on a prop or an item all carry and land through, and which `EffectSpec` is the authored form of, readable by any content file including a game's own; and `Ignite`, `Emit` and `Noise` registered by the fire, gas and noise plugins, which depends on combat and statuses so neither depends on abilities, and a game registers its own with `add_effect`. A carrier's `EffectBonus`, which the game writes when it spawns the thing with whatever made it stronger already applied, is copied onto the `Landing`, and `Harm` and `Mend` add its amount to their roll and `Inflict` its turns, so the engine never learns what an enchant level is. Every engine effect refuses an argument it does not name. An effect asks for damage, a status or a move through `EffectWorld`, and reaches anything else through `Commands`.
- Places: bounded maps entered by transitions or warps, built on first arrival, kept whole, off-map actors frozen; the map being read follows the player, and anyone else goes through a `Transition` only with `Wits::TRAVELS`, behind whoever its `Trails` names when it stood beside them as they left, landing on the nearest free cell, while a `WarpRequest` moves anyone; `Travelled` reports every change of map and `PlaceEntered::first` is the player's first arrival, whoever built the place; `PlaceBuild::from_context` reads a finished chain, each stamped mark a `Spot` carrying the key of the piece it came from, and a mark under a later stamp dropped, since the piece on top owns its cells; `WarpRequest::into_place` starts a run in one.
- `layout::passes` turns one recipe of a loaded layout file into the passes that grow it, `Accrete`, `OpenLoops` and `Require`, with the size of the map to run them on; no plugin and no system, as `Prefabs::piece` has none.
- Prefab slots, opt-in by adding `PrefabPlugin<A, I>`: `Prefabs` holds a game's prefab files and roles behind one shared copy, hands its chain a keyed piece by name with `piece`, and says what a stamped spot stands for with `slot`. On the arrival that built a place, every keyed spot's slot draws from a stream derived for its own cell and then spawns, unless it is the arrival cell, a cell no longer walkable or one already filled that arrival: a prop through `spawn_prop`, items through the game's `ItemMaker` as `Found::Placed`, and a monster through `ActorMaker`, the trait beside it on the resource holding a game's monsters, with its spawn table, a place's band, `make_at` making one with nothing rolled, which `make` calls unless a game rolls something, and `id_of` and `name_of` for a save, and a `Post` on its cell. A `Mark` and every spot of an unkeyed piece stay the game's.
- The surface is optional, and everything regional belongs to it: `WorldMap::new` takes the tile tables alone, the region size is read from the world graph when the first window loads, and `Knowledge` is initialised by the engine. A delve names neither.
- Knowledge: explored tiles per map in buckets of its own, and the surface's seen regions and discovered sites kept apart from them, so going underground never hides the overworld's fog. Beside each map's explored tiles, a `Recalled` glyph and colour for every tile a still thing was last seen on, written by the map view through `see_still` and read through `recalled`, kept aside with the map on a switch and saved; `learn` marks a tile known without sight, as a home town is, and has the next look take in what stands on it.
- Statuses ticked by the turn through the damage pipeline, one put on for `UNTIL_CURED` turns never counted down, one put on again while still on reported as `StatusEvent::Renewed` rather than `Applied`, and one that holds or sleeps spending each of its holder's turns as a wait, by `pass_held_turns` in `DecideSet::Sense`, before its mind or its player is asked. A status that sleeps ends itself the moment its holder is woken, by harm, by a noise with its `Hearing` threshold left, or by a foe it notices within `SLEEPER_REACH`, two cells, by its chance there alone; `Sleepers::asleep` answers who is asleep. `StatusPlugin` gives every actor an empty `Afflicted` and `StatBlock` as it is spawned, so a monster spawned without them still takes a status, and a game without statuses carries neither.
- Every actor requires a `Speed`, normal unless the spawn says otherwise, and `TurnRules::new().speed_stat(stat)` names a stat whose value is added to it as a percentage wherever the turn loop reads it, through `Speeds`, the one reader the sheet and the inspect forecast read too, so haste and slowness are statuses on a stat.
- Stealth, opt-in by adding `StealthPlugin`: `Notice` on observers and `Stealth` on subjects, `Aware` remembering who has noticed whom, a roll to notice in `DecideSet::Notice` for the actor about to decide, waking on a blow in `TurnSet::React`, and a `Noticed` message on the flip; the minds act only on hiders they have noticed and search where they last saw them; a status registered `unseen` puts `Unseen` on its holder, whom no mind perceives and no observer notices at any distance, until it runs out or its holder makes an attack, a blow or shot, a throw, or an ability on anyone else, which ends it before the damage lands; the roll is from `StealthRng`, stealth's own stream, and works without combat, everyone being at odds where there are no sides. `Watchers::unaware` answers yes for a sleeping status's holder to whoever strikes it, with or without the plugin running, and a sleeper's own noticing is narrowed to `SLEEPER_REACH` and rolled only on its turn, so sleep and stealth share the one roll rather than opening a second.
- Noise, opt-in by adding `NoisePlugin::new(NoiseRules)`: the engine's own actions make it, a step from `Stepped` (the move resolver's message for every step it lets through) as loud as the stepper's `Footfall` or the rule, a blow once per attacker where it stands, a door, and a thrown thing where it lands; a game writes a `MakeNoise` for anything else, with a `SoundId` interned by `Sounds` and `add_sound`, or makes one from content with the `Noise` effect, one noise by the landing's user where it came down, its sound resolved at load through `SoundNames`; every noise of a pass flooded once in `TurnSet::Listen`, after the pass's reactions, over a square bounded by its loudness and clipped to the loaded window; a `NoiseHeard` for every listener with `Hearing` it reaches with enough left, the player included, carrying how much loudness was still on it when it arrived; and `Heard`, an `Awareness` of the loudest place each listener heard, aged in `DecideSet::Notice` and forgotten after its memory; `wake_on_noise` cures a sleeping status on whoever the noise reached. A listener hears a place and never who made it, except that it does not hear itself, follows it as a trail unless it can see the cell, and stealth and hearing offer their trails to `Thinking::offer_trail`, which puts the freshest in `Snapshot::last_known`.
- Lighting, opt-in by adding `LightingPlugin`, which inserts a `Lighting` of its own, dark until the game says otherwise: `LightSource` on a prop, an actor or an item, shed from the carrier once carried; static and dynamic layers recast only when their sources change, once a frame in `EngineSet::Light` and inside the turn loop in `DecideSet::Light` in any pass since a light changed, so a mind sees by the light as it is on its own turn, with the viewsheds marked stale by a move bounded to the discs of the sources that moved rather than all of them; the map's `opacity_epoch` so an edit that changes what blocks sight refreshes light and every viewshed without anyone moving; `DarkSight`; `Fuel` ticked by the turn with `LightEvent::BurntOut`; the viewshed keeps its geometric `line` and its seen `visible`, and minds perceive along a line only what is lit, within their dark sight or adjacent.
- Gas, opt-in by adding `GasPlugin`: a field per gas per map in `Gases`, given off by `Release` and `Vents` with what a cell cannot hold spilling to the nearest cells, stepped every whole turn over what does not stop a thrown thing with its swirls hashed from the seed and the turn, written into the map's veil where thick enough so sight and light stop there, and breathed, as `Breathed` and the gas's status.
- Fire, opt-in by adding `FirePlugin`: `Fire` per map, set alight by `Kindle` and by what is `Burning`, fed by burning tiles, `Flammable` things and gas that burns, stepped every whole turn from hashed rolls; burnt ground becomes the tile it leaves, whoever stands in it is `Scorched` and given `FireRules::inflicts`, and burning cells smoke and glow through `Lighting::set_glow`. `FireEvent` reports what the game answers. Minds will not step into fire.
- Props, opt-in by adding `PropsPlugin`: what stands on a map that is neither an actor nor an item, as content. `PropDef` in a `props.ron` (look, whether it blocks, what breaks it, what it offers, what it holds, what sets it off, how hard it is to spot) loaded into `Registries::props`; `spawn_prop` puts one where the game says, and `rl-render`'s `dress_props` gives it the glyph its definition asks for, so nothing below the renderer names a colour. `Verbs` interns what an interaction is called, `open` and `search` being the engine's and `add_verb` a game's; `OfferedHere` is the gate, worked out in `DecideSet::Offer` for whoever holds the turn over what it stands on and stands beside, each offer carrying its cost and, when something is wanted, the tag that refuses it; `Interact { target, verb }` is the one action, refused for free when the gate did not offer it, and `Interacted` is how a game answers a verb of its own in `TurnSet::React`. An actor offers the same way through its own `Offers`, a list of `Offering { verb, time, needs }`, never to its own holder nor to anyone it is hostile to by `CombatRules`, so a captive to free or a person to talk to is offered and refused by the one rule the interact key, the offers screen and the bump agree on. A bump into a prop or an actor offering exactly one thing comes to an interaction, beside the bump that comes to opening a door, and `Bumped::offers` is what two or a refused one offered, which `OffersPanel` opens on. A `Container` is `Inventory` on a prop, stocked by `FillContainer` (the engine rolls the counts from `PropRng`; a row names an item or asks for a tag, drawn from the game's loot table at the container's band plus an offset, and `LootPlugin` answers both through the game's `ItemMaker`), emptied through `Take`, which merges stacks the way the ground does, and marked `Emptied` when its definition says what an emptied one looks like, which `rl-render` then redresses it as. A prop's `triggers` are the `Triggers` an item carries, answering `entered`, reported from `Stepped` at the end of the pass's travel and landed in that same pass, or `destroyed`, reported from the prop's own `DeathEvent` on a `Remnant` and landed the pass after, as many times as each one's `fires` says, counted on the prop, and landed as the prop's own doing rather than as whoever set it off; what they land is engine effects, built on the first frame, so a trap is data and a game reads `Fired` only for what no effect can say. `Hidden { spot }` keeps a prop out of the map view and out of `InSight` until the player's per-turn roll from `PropRng` finds it, and a hidden trap still springs. A definition that offers, holds, answers and hides nothing and cannot be broken is scenery, `PropDef::is_scenery`, and its prop carries `Scenery`, which `InSight` leaves out as well, so machinery that says what a room was for is drawn and named under the look cursor but never a row in the nearby rail or a stop on Tab. `Snapshot::props` is `PropView { id, pos, side }` for everything in sight that is neither actor nor item, bodies included and unspotted props never, filled in `PerceiveSet::Annotate` and asking no wits. Props are saved by the engine itself, by definition name plus each one's own history. A prop with `health` is attacked and killed like anything else. `docs/design/props.md` is the reasoning.
- Remains, opt-in by adding `RemainsPlugin` and, per actor, `LeavesRemains`: the dead actor is kept rather than despawned, with `WasLiving` taken off it (`Dead`, `Actor`, `Blocks`, `Health`, `Mind`, `Perception`, `Viewshed`, what it noticed and heard, and its `Post`) and `Remains { since, credit }` put on, so everything a game spawned it with is still on the same entity under the same save kind, and nothing of what died is guessed at. A `RemainsLeft` for a game to answer in `TurnSet::React`, `Prop` on the body so it is seen, listed and offered verbs like anything else standing in a cell, `RemainsNaming` as the one template that names it (`"{what} remains"` by default, applied on conversion and again on restore), and `EntityState::remains` so a continued run lays them back down through the same `lay_down`. The moment it dies, the actor is copied whole onto a `Disabled` twin linked by `Life`, which no ordinary query sees and which is despawned the moment the body stops being remains; a component that is not `Clone` is named in an error then. `revive`, or `commands.revive`, makes the body its twin again: the twin's values for everything but where it lies and what it carries, never a looted item and never a bag taken away, on the nearest free cell within `STANDING_ROOM` when something stands on it, with `Revived` sent. The engine never removes them of its own accord, never names them, and says nothing about what they are for; `docs/design/remains.md` is the reasoning.
- Work, part of `CorePlugin`: an actor doing one thing across many turns. `rl_rules::work` has `Work`, counted in the worker's own turns, `Progress` and `in_reach`, one cell; a mind begins work with `Decision::Work`, anything else with `Works::begin`, and every turn after is claimed before the mind is asked and spent as a wait, until `WorkDone`. `break_work` ends it with `WorkBroken` when the worker is hurt, even by a hit healed in the same pass, dies, or can no longer reach its target, when another worker finishes the same target, or when the game calls `Works::stop`. Kinds are words declared with `add_work`, which is what a panel shows; `EntityState::working` keeps it in a save. `docs/design/work.md` is the reasoning.
- `MapFields`, what fire and gas keep per map: the current map's field fitted to the window, every other map's set aside, and every one saved.
- Facts fed to quests and counters after the frame, and both put back as a fresh run finds them in `EndRun`, so either may be inserted once for every run.
- Save exports for the scheduler, the world's edits and places, and knowledge.
- New actors are admitted to the queue with the player first and the rest in spawn order, so the first turn of a run never depends on the order a query happens to walk the archetypes in.
  An actor is admitted when it first stands where play is, on the current map inside the loaded window, so a player spawned at the origin and warped onto its first map is admitted beside the monsters its arrival put there, not frozen a step or two behind them.
- A headless app for tests, and `testing`, what goes into one: `surface` stands a `TestWorld` up and hands back open ground to start on, `two_sides` inserts combat rules for two sides at war, and `KeyScriptPlugin` with `press` plays keys the way a keyboard does. One copy for the engine's crates and a game's tests alike, where there had been nine copies of the world and three of the key player.
- A fingerprint tripwire, `tests/fingerprint.rs`: a seeded run of minds, combat, stealth and items hashed to one number, so a change that moves a roll, a decision or an order fails a test rather than drifting a replay. Re-baselined on purpose, with a changelog line, when a change means to move it.
- Settings: `Settings`, a registry of what a player chooses once, each a `Setting` of a name, a group, a label, its choices, a default and an optional key, declared with `app.add_setting` for a `SettingId`. Plain data in the one crate the renderer, the UI and saving all depend on, so whoever owns what a setting changes declares it and reads it, the settings screen lists it and saving remembers it, with no edge between the three. `remembered` and `recall` are the map of names to the words of their choices that saving writes and reads.

### rl-render

- A diffed terminal back buffer.
- A game's own typeface: `TerminalPlugin::new(width, height, cell_size, font_size)`, and `typeface` with the bytes of a TrueType or OpenType file the game ships, compiled in, which every glyph is then drawn in; without one, glyphs come from the system's monospace family, another face on every platform, and on the web from the font Bevy embeds, which is ASCII alone. Bytes rather than an asset path, since an asset arrives after the first frames and on the web only from a page that serves it.
- A grid laid out for its window: `layout::fit` gives the largest whole-pixel cells that fit the grid in a window of any size and the margin that centres them, and the terminal lays itself out again whenever the window changes, with glyphs drawn at the size they are shown, where a camera scaling the grid stretched glyphs drawn for another size. At the window a game opens in, the declared cell exactly, wherever that is a whole number of the display's pixels.
- `Tileset`: pictures in place of glyphs, as a font is to a terminal. A resource naming a sheet, how it is cut up, a picture per character and, with `within`, the part of the terminal it covers; `flush_terminal` draws a cell whose glyph it names as that picture, tinted by the cell's colour, with no glyph, and every other cell as before, so the map, the light on it, memory, fire and gas, particles, the cursors and a game's own overlays are drawn in pictures with no second drawing path. `turned(false)`, or removing it, is the same game in glyphs. One sprite a cell, spawned the first time there is a tileset and laid out with the grid. `docs/design/tilesets.md` is the reasoning.
- Tiles wider than a cell: `MapViewPlugin::across(n)`, or `RoguelikePlugins::across(n)`, draws each map tile `n` cells across, so a terminal of narrow cells, where text reads as text, shows square tiles. `MapView` is what knows: `to_screen` is a tile's first cell, `to_world` takes any of its cells back, `cells` and `tiles` walk them, and `set`, `wash` and `put` write a tile whole, which `draw_map`, the particles, the cursors and the panel highlights call. `set` writes a tile as one wide cell of the terminal, `Terminal::set_wide`, its glyph or picture drawn centred across the run until something else is written into it, so a screen over the map is drawn a letter to a cell. One cell to a tile unless a game says otherwise.
- `Pointer`: the terminal cell the mouse is over, kept by `TerminalPlugin` once a frame after the grid is laid out, through `Fit::cell_at`, the layout's own arithmetic run backwards, so it is right in a window of any size and `None` in the margin; `within(rect)` is the cell when it is inside a panel's rectangle and `tile(&view)` the map tile a `MapView` draws there. What a click means is the game's, read from Bevy's mouse buttons. With no window it is left where a test put it.
- `FullscreenPlugin`: filling the screen as a setting, `fullscreen`, `Off` or `On`, with F11 as its key where a `SettingsPanel` is there to read it, which keeps the primary window borderless fullscreen or windowed to match and puts a choice already made on the window before the first frame. Declares nothing on the web, where a browser grants fullscreen only from inside a key's handler.
- The map view with lit, remembered and unknown tiles. `MapViewPlugin::new(rect)` takes the rectangle it draws in, the way every panel does, and needs field of view, since without it nothing is ever seen. A remembered tile shows the still thing last seen on it, an item, a prop or a way through, faded as the tile is: `remember_still_things` takes the topmost glyph of everything in sight that is neither an `Actor` nor `Hidden` into `Knowledge` once a frame, so what comes or goes out of sight changes nothing until its tile is seen again, and a creature is never remembered.
- `TileAppearance::load`: how every tile looks, from a RON list against the tile registry, every unknown, doubled or missing tile reported at once, so a game's colours are a file beside its monsters.
- Shading, in the manner of Brogue: each tile authored with both colours and a `Vary` that jitters every cell by a hash of its position and can shimmer over time; light multiplies glyph and background channel by channel, down to a dark floor and up to a gain cap; the wavering part of a light dips on a smooth noise so flames ripple; `Memory` fades what was seen to a darker, greyer, cooler colour.
- `LightOverlay`: intensity drawn as digits.
- `FieldAppearance`: flames over every burning cell in sight, flickering cell by cell with embers rising off them, and the ground tinted by the densest gas on it, drawn as haze, heavier the thicker it is, where it hides what is behind it.
- `ShownFields`, what the map draws of fire and gas: each cell blending over a third of a second toward what its fields hold, from when the last blast playing reaches it, so a cloud rolls on a turn and a grenade's smoke shows behind its blast; stirred by `churn`, noise over the wall clock, so a hanging cloud moves; and a cloud in sight drawn whole over known ground. `Wavefronts` is where each blast's front is, written by the particles.
- Particles: `Particles` plays `Animation`s of `Trail` (a glyph flying between two anchors with a fading tail, the line drawn again each frame so a target that moves bends the flight) and `Burst` (every anchor lit at once and fading, each one of a few glyphs, or with a source a blast: ring by ring out from it with a ragged front, white-hot cooling to embers, lighting the ground, and throwing sparks ahead) steps from the wall clock over the map. `ParticlesPlugin`, in `RoguelikePlugins`, plays every cue the turns write, one animation per actor per frame in the order written, in the ability's `look`, the item's glyph, a look given outright such as an attack's, or `ParticleStyle`'s plain one; watches the `TurnHold` unless the style is `instant`, holding the turns while anything that holds them plays and there is a turn to wait for, and skipping through every hold in the way on any key pressed, with the turns run on to the player's own before the game reads the key; and leaves an animation behind on the map it played on.
- `CapturePlugin`: `RL_CAPTURE` plays `RL_CAPTURE_KEYS`, letters, marks and named keys down to `\` and `backspace`, through the real keyboard input, photographs the window without taking focus, refuses a black frame, and exits; `RL_CAPTURE_AT=hold` shoots into the first hold on the turns after the keys, so the flight or the burst the keys caused is what is photographed.
- Glyph entities filtered to the current map.

### rl-ui

Every panel splits three ways: a view (a resource of plain data), a collector (the system that rebuilds it each frame, in `ViewSet::Collect`), and a presenter (one way of drawing it, in a `PresentSet` layer).
`ViewSet::Sight` runs before `Collect` and fills `Sighted`, what the player can see in list order, which the nearby, inspect and target collectors read rather than each scanning and sorting for themselves; it is filled again before the keys are read, since the turns run between the two moments.
The query is the half a game reuses; the drawing is the half it may replace or drop.
Opt-in is per panel, and a presenter pulls its view plugin in behind it.

Which is why the view plugins are named here: a game that writes its own presenter over a view the engine already collects adds the view plugin itself, and needs it by name.

- `UiPlugin` owns the `MessageLog`, because a game writes to the log from its own systems whether or not it draws it: a headless test adds the plugin and has a log with no panel. A line may carry `Span`s, runs of its text in the colour of the thing they name, which the log strip and the scrollback draw through `readable`, lifting a dark colour toward the text colour until it clears the surface. `Tones::HIT` and `Tones::KILL` are the two built-in tones brighter than text, for the player's blows and kills. The log is cleared in `EndRun`.
- The narrator: `NarrationView`, rows of `Said` (its `Words`, an engine `Phrase` or a game's own template and tone, who, whom, what, an amount, a registry name, where, and whether the player saw it), collected in `TurnSet::Record`, a phase of its own after every reaction and before cleanup, one pass at a time so a frame of many turns reads in the order they happened, a notice by the actor holding the turn read before what that actor then did; a game's own line, a `Tell` with a template, a tone and whom it names, written from inside the pass and collected after the pass's events as a row whose `Words` are the game's own; and `NarratorPlugin`, which speaks each row in `ViewSet::Speak` through a `Phrasebook` of one template and tone per phrase, split by perspective (`YouHit`, `HitsYou`, `OthersFight`, `YouKill`, `Dies`, `YouPickUp`, `YouUseThing`, `YourUseHits`, `Wields`, `OpensDoor`, `NoticesYou`, `YouUse`, `YouUseOn`, `YouUseOnMany`, `YouAreAfflicted`, `WillNotComeOff`, `YourLightGoesOut`, and the rest), with names coloured as the things they name, what the player puts on, wields, takes off or drops said as theirs through `{your what}`, a thing carrying `MassNoun` said with no `a`, harm an ability or a thing landed named by what landed it, a use named by whom it hit only when it is aimed at someone and caught exactly one, several caught by a ground-aimed use still counted, and a status told when it begins and not while it is kept up.
A use a `Reflects` body turns back writes `Reflected { by, onto, source }`, said before the harm it caused as `YouReflect`, `ReflectsOntoYou` or `OthersReflect`, so a caster hurt by its own cast is never read with no reason first. A game rewords or silences any phrase, speaks the unseen or not, edits rows in `ViewSet::Annotate`, or reads the view itself.
- `CursorStyle`, how a screen marks the cell it points at, and `cursor::mark`, the one drawing of it: `Glow { tone, pulse }` washes the cell and leaves what stands there showing, `Ticks { marks, tone, pulse }` frames it with four marks and never covers it. Both take a `ToneId` and an optional second tone to breathe toward, at the pulse `cursor::pulse` sets. `InspectPanel`, `NearbyPanel` and `TargetPanel` each take one in their constructor, so a game picks one mark and picks it everywhere; the defaults are ticks for the look cursor and a glow for the rail's highlight and the aim.
- `OffersPanel`: the screen that asks which of several things you meant, with `OffersView` behind it, kept by `OffersViewPlugin`. It opens itself when the interact key finds more than one offer in reach and when a bump into a prop that offers more than one is refused, walks its rows on the direction keys, takes the one picked out on confirm, and lists what cannot be taken up greyed with the reason in words. A row reads as the verb's registered name and the prop's own name, so the engine still invents no words.
- The interact key, `UiPlugin`'s in a game with `PropsPlugin`: the key that does what is here, for what a bump cannot reach: a body underfoot, a plate already found, a prop that does not block. It takes the one offer in reach, and where several are offered it opens `OffersPanel` to ask, or does nothing in a game with no such screen. `InteractKeys` binds it, `Enter` by default.
- `PlayerKeysPlugin`: the standard keys, for the verbs a game names, each declared under the game's own words and read in `PlayerKeysSet`, inside `EngineSet::Input`, while no screen is up: `walk` a `Bump`, `go_through` a `GoThrough`, `wait` a `Wait`, `pick_up` a `PickUp`, `fire` an `AimFire` when the player has a ranged attack, and `throw` the pack opened on the first thing that can be thrown, or the first tagged as `throw_first` names. Fire or throw with nothing to act on spends no turn, says the game's `Otherwise` line if it gave one, and writes a `Declined` for a game that says why from its own state, as Foundry does for a hot or dry gun. A game's own verbs stay in its own system, ordered against the set.
- `ContainerPanel`: the screen onto what you opened, run end to end by the engine. It opens itself on the engine's `Interacted` with the `open` verb, the direction keys walk the rows, the cursors' confirm key takes the one picked out and `a` takes everything, and it closes on the cursors' close key or as soon as the container is out of reach. Take-only. `ContainerView` is its view, filled from `OpenContainer`, which is the screen's own state rather than the world's.
- `GameMenuPanel`: the menu on Escape while playing, and the screen a run ends on, opened by the engine on `EngineState::Over` under the game's words for death or victory with the epitaph and the run's own line from its record, each when there is one, the seed and the turn. Back to the run, a new run, the same seed again, the settings, Main menu once a `TitlePanel` was added, or quit; a new run and the same seed are one `Restart` each, and Main menu is one `LeaveRun`. Under those it draws the sections of the record `RecordsPlugin` filed for the run and every section a game pushed onto `EndingView` in `ViewSet::Annotate`, each under its heading, clipped to the rectangle the game gave it.
- `TitlePanel`: the screen before there is a run, the engine's menu over a picture the game paints in `TitleSet::Backdrop`. It holds the first run back with `HoldStart` and offers Continue, in a game that keeps a save slot, over a save this build can read, New Game, which asks before it gives a save up, Records once a records screen is declared, Settings when there is something to set, and Exit, drawing a row dim while it cannot be taken; Continue inserts `ContinueRun`, Yes to the question triggers `AbandonRun`, and both begin the run through `begin_run`. It knows nothing of storage, reading `SavedRun`, and its words are the game's through `TitleWords`. `docs/guide/src/systems/title.md` is the reference.
- `RecordsPanel`: every run's record, best score first, as `rank. score  summary  date`, scrolled to keep the row picked out in view; confirm opens one, its summary over its seed and turn and its sections drawn the way the end screen draws them, and close goes back a level. It declares the `records` modal the title's Records row opens.
- `SettingsPanel`: the settings screen, every declared setting under its group with its choice at the right edge, the direction keys to pick a row and change it and the close key to go back. It is read and drawn outside the engine's sets, after the whole of `EngineSet::Present` and in any engine state, so a game opens it from a title screen of its own before there is a world. The menu offers it as a `Settings` row above `Quit` when the panel was added and a setting is declared, and is not drawn under it; a setting with a key is a control the controls screen lists.
- Tones: a semantic role interned as a `ToneId` over a `Palette` a game extends with roles the engine never heard of, with `add_tone(name, colour)` declaring and colouring one while the app is built, and a warning by name at startup for any left without a colour. No widget takes a `Color`.
- Facets: `Facet { key, text, tone }` pushed onto a row in `ViewSet::Annotate`, so a game's vocabulary reaches a panel without an engine type learning a word.
- Views and their collectors: `NearbyView` (actors and things in the viewshed, in the order `InSight` lists them, with health, a relation, the row picked out, and `Alert`: whether something is hunting you, searching after a noise or after you once you went unseen, or aware of nothing, which the engine reads off stealth and hearing and refuses to name; `AlertWords` on the panel is what each is called), `VitalsView` (bars, armor, marked uncertain while anything worn is not yet known, which a game clears for what it knows cannot move the figure, status badges, turn, position, whether anything is watching, `exposure`: the `LightBand` the player stands in, `None` without lighting, and `noise`: how loud it is where the player stands, the loudest arrival of the turn, falling away over the turns after it by `NoiseFade` so a noise reads as a room going quiet rather than as a light switching off, and `None` for a game without noise or a player without ears), `GearView` (every registered slot, filled or not, a worn thing's charges unless it is not yet known, `stuck` for one that will not come off, and a fed weapon's rounds as an `ammo` facet), `InspectView` (the look cursor's subject, said to be unknown when it is not yet known, or what the game says it seems in `seems`, a duel forecast that counts each side's misses and each side's speed as the turns deal it, the chance the player's own attack lands with the lines behind it, and the ground under the cursor: the tile's name, whether it burns, what gas it is in), `TargetView` (the ability, thing's use, throw or shot being aimed, the footprint it would cover, whether the resolver would accept it and why not, and what is under it, with the reach of a thing not yet known left out; a status's modifiers are named after the status and a worn item's after the item's `Name`, and a game names sources of its own through `name_source`).
- `InventoryView`: the player's bag in pickup order, each `ItemRow` with its name and glyph, its count, the slot it is worn in, the slots it could go on and any it takes as well, and its worth read off its own components in the registries' names: the blow, the shot, the extra strikes, the armor, what it resists, what it bestows, how far it flies and what it strikes for thrown; what its triggers do, in their effects' own words with its `EffectBonus` added, how its use is aimed and what it asks for; its charges, when the next comes back and whether it is attuned; and whether it is unknown, which keeps its name and where it goes and drops its worth, with what the game says it seems, `seems`, printed in place of the bare word, and whether it is stuck. Anything else about it is the game's, pushed as a facet.
- Panels: `NearbyPanel`, `VitalsPanel`, `GearPanel`, `InspectPanel` (the cursor as four pulsing ASCII ticks on the tiles around the one looked at, which keeps its own glyph, a dash either side and a bar above and below, each centred on its tile; how far off its subject is, `here` for what lies underfoot; each of its facets wrapped on spaces onto as many lines as it takes rather than cut with an ellipsis, those with no room counted on a muted last row, `InspectPanel::more("{n} more")` by default; and the panel moved to the mirror of its rectangle, or to where `else_at` says, while any cell of the cursor's tile or of a tile a tick stands on would sit under it), `LogPanel`, `ScrollbackPanel`, `TargetPanel` (the footprint on the map, and a framed box with the range, the target, the chance to hit and every line behind it, or why the aim is refused, drawn only while the cursor is up), `AbilityPanel`, `InventoryPanel`, `ControlsPanel` and `SheetPanel`, each a plugin holding its rectangle and its headings.
- `InventoryPanel`: the bag run end to end, opened by `InventoryKeys`, walked with the direction keys, the row picked out described under the list, and four keys that act on it: wear or take off, drop and use write the engine's own item intents, use opens the targeting cursor through `AimUse` for a thing whose use is aimed and turns the bag to choosing, under the use's prompt, for one that asks for a thing, throw opens the targeting cursor through `AimThrow`, none is offered that would take a stuck thing off, and every action closes every screen since it spends the turn. A bag taller than its rectangle counts the rows above and below its list in the muted tone. A game names it with `title`, `called` and `empty`, and its keys are listed on the controls screen under that title.
- The targeting overlay: the flight in the notice tone with a pulse running from the user to the cursor, the cells hit in the select tone or the bad one when refused, the cursor brightest, and the part of the line past where the aim stops in the bad tone, so out of reach reads as out of reach.
- `AbilityPanel`: the ability menu run end to end, opened by `AbilityKeys`, walked with the direction keys, a row confirmed with the cursor's confirm key writes `AimAt` for it, and the row picked out described under the list.
- Modals: a screen closed by a key counts as open for the rest of that frame, so the key is not read again as the world's; the targeting cursor is not steered on the frame it opens, and the bag reads none of its keys on the frame it opens, for the same reason.
- `SheetPanel`: the character sheet as a modal, opened by `SheetKeys` (`c` by default, and listed on the controls screen), drawn a section at a time with a section that has nothing in it left out.
- `controls`: `Controls`, the registry every key is declared in as a group, an action and its `Keys`; `ControlInput`, a frame's keys read through it by `ControlId`, with `which` for a slot row, `direction` and `direction_held` for the direction keys, and `label` for what a player reads; `Repeats` and `RepeatPace`, a held direction key read by `direction` and by both cursors as pressed again after a quarter second and every twelfth after, so every walk, cursor and shove repeats and no game keeps its own timer; `Chord`, a key with Shift matched exactly. An `EngineKey` names one of the engine's own bindings and is read from `DirectionKeys`, `CursorKeys`, `ScrollbackKeys` or `ControlsKeys` when listed, so a rebound key is listed as rebound. Every engine plugin with keys declares them in `finish`, in shared words, so a control two plugins read is listed once and a game's groups come first.
- `ControlsPanel`: the registry as a screen, groups in declaration order flowed into columns and then pages, a heading never ending a column, opened and closed by `ControlsKeys` (`?` and Escape, the arrows for pages), with an optional one-row hint naming the key that opens it.
- The scrollback: the whole log on a modal screen, wrapped rather than clipped, ruled off per turn, scrolled by line and by page with both ends clamped, and filtered by cycling only the tones the log actually holds. A second presenter over the same `MessageLog` the strip draws, with its cursor and filter in a `Scrollback` resource of its own.
- `focus`: `InSight`, what the player can see, actors nearest first and then things nearest first, ties by name and then by entity, which is the one list the nearby panel prints and both cursors cycle; and `Focus`, the one entity picked out of it. With nothing open, Tab steps the focus down the list, Shift with it steps back, and Escape lets go, before it would open the menu. `NearbyPanel` draws the row picked out and its map tile on the selection tone, scrolling to keep the row on the rail, and either cursor opens on it when it can take it.
- `cursor`: what the two cursors share. `CursorKeys` is the one set of bindings both answer to, the nearby list included, and `steer` the one reading of a frame's keys: close over confirm over moving, the candidates asked for only when the cursor moves, cycled entity by entity with a wrap both ways so two things on one tile are two stops, the `Focus` moved onto whatever the cursor lands or steps on, and a step that never leaves the loaded window.
- The look cursor: opens on the focus or the top of the list, steps with the direction keys, cycles actors and things in sight, describes the one picked out of two on a tile, stays inside the loaded window, and owns input as a modal.
- The targeting cursor: a game writes `AimAt` for an ability, `AimUse` for a thing used at somewhere, `AimThrow` for something carried or `AimFire` for a shot, and the engine does the rest; `AimUse` uses at once a thing whose use lands on its user, opens the bag choosing for one that asks, and opens nothing for one that cannot be used now, so a game's use key writes it for anything. It cycles what the aim can take by `Aim::cycles_to`, so an aim at the ground stops on anything in sight rather than on cells. A throw opens on what is picked out, then on what the last aim was fired at, `LastAimed`, then on the nearest foe; put away, fired or not, it leaves nothing picked out. It previews through `flight` who it strikes first and where it lands, and writes the `Throw` on confirm; the banner says what is aimed by the name the view carries. A shot previews through `shot`, the line `line_of_fire` answers by, and writes an `Attack` on whoever stands under the cursor.
  It opens on the nearest thing worth aiming at by `Aim::worth_aiming_at`, the rule a mind's tactic aims by, previews through `Bystanders::land`, the call the resolver lands the use with, and writes the `Use` intent on confirm.
  A property test over seeded layouts holds the preview to it: who the banner lists and whether it reads as refused are what the resolver does.
  An ability that wants no cursor is used at once, so a game binds every ability the same way.
  The overlay repaints the backgrounds the map already drew, keeping every glyph: the cells hit, the flight to them, and the whole footprint in the bad tone with the reason in the banner when the resolver would refuse.
- Awareness and hearing on the panels: `Row::alert` is `Unaware`, `Searching` or `Hunting` for each actor in sight, hunting outranking searching, read off stealth's watchers and off `Heard`, and `None` for something that neither notices nor hears or in a game with neither stealth nor noise; the rail writes it after the name in the game's own words through `AlertWords` and mutes an actor that knows of nothing, clipping a long name from the end and keeping the state whole after it rather than clipping the two together unless the row is too narrow to keep four characters of the name beside it, and `VitalsView::seen` reads hidden or seen.
- Work on the panels: `Row::work` is a `WorkRow`, what an actor is working at, the target's name and the turns left, filled by any collector through `Workings`; the rail writes the work's word where it would write the alert, and inspect adds a line from `InspectPanel::working`'s templates.
- What a monster is doing on the panels: a `Mind` carries `Doing`, the tactic that decided its last turn, and `Row::doing` carries it to the rail, which writes the game's word for a tactic it named through `NearbyPanel::activities(ActivityWords)` in place of the alert word, after any work word, so one running from the player reads fleeing rather than hunting; a tactic left unnamed reads as the alert.
- `ReplayPlugin`, in `RoguelikePlugins`: `RL_RECORD=run.ron` writes every frame's keys down, the held-key repeat turned into the presses it stood for, and the file with each; `RL_REPLAY=run.ron` presses them again, each when the player holds a turn, the turns are not held for a cue, and the clock reads what it read then, and stops with the key's number when the game has drifted past a recorded clock. A test names its file and its recording outright.
- `Modals`: a stack of interned modal ids with `modal_is`, `modal_open` and `no_modal` run conditions, so one gate covers every screen a game adds, declared with `add_modal(name)` by the engine's own screens and a game's alike. The cursors' close key closes whatever is on top when nothing else answered it that frame, after every screen has read its keys, so a screen a game forgot to teach to close still closes and one that closes itself is never closed twice.
- `DirectionKeys`: arrows, vi keys and the numpad to the eight directions, in one resource a game may replace.
- A message log carrying a tone per line, folding a repeat into a count, filterable by tone.
- A framed scrolling list menu.
- Drawing helpers a game writing its own presenter reuses: frames, section headings, bars, clipping, word wrapping, and rectangle splits.

### rl-overworld

- `OverworldPlugin`, the world map as a screen, opt-in like any other. No example game adds it or `StreamingPlugin` yet.
- Says what it needs: no layout, no screen, and it says so when play begins rather than drawing nothing.
- Open or closed on the shared `Modals` stack, so it and a game's own screens cannot both think they own the arrow keys.
- The world drawn from its bands with rivers, roads, discovered sites and the player.
- A portal picker.

### rl-save

- Backends for files, memory and browser storage behind one resource.
- An exact-match versioned envelope.
- Entity remapping.
- The run, saved by kind: a game implements `Saveable` on the component that marks a kind of thing it spawns, saying how to write one down and spawn one again, and `SaveableState` on a resource it keeps of the run, and registers each with `save_kind` and `save_state`; an item made through the game's `ItemMaker` may instead implement `SaveableItem`, registered with `save_items`, which the engine writes as a `SavedItem` of its definition's name and its `ItemLevel` and makes again through `ItemMaker::make_at` before the game's own extra goes back on, and an actor made through its `ActorMaker` may implement `SaveableActor`, registered with `save_actors`, written as a `SavedActor` of its definition's name and made again, nowhere, through `ActorMaker::make_at` before its extra goes back on; the engine walks the world into a `RunSave`, every kind's entries in the kind's own RON, and captures and puts back what it owns on each entity in `EntityState`: where it stands, its health, its bag, its slots (re-worn by each item's shape), its statuses (re-inflicted by request), its stack, its transition, its `Post`, how long it has left alight if it is `Burning`, the work it is in the middle of, dropped on load when what it worked on was not saved, and the one it follows as its leader, by save id, dropped the same way when that one was not saved. `Quests` saves its tracker, `Counters` its fact ledger and `Scattered` the streamed regions whose loot has been put down, out of the box. Remains are saved as what they were, with a prop kind laid on them recorded as part of the remains, and one entity claimed by two kinds refuses the capture. `SavePlugin` keeps the `Stash` a turn behind the run, deletes the slot when the run ends, and checks the game's version and the engine's `RunSave::FORMAT`; for a title it inserts `SavedRun`, reads the slot into it before the first frame and keeps it current, forgets the slot when `AbandonRun` is triggered, and writes a run left with `LeaveRun` down as it stands, through `save_on_leaving`, before its teardown, which then keeps the slot for Continue; `.on_arrival()` also writes the slot on every frame the player arrived somewhere, so each place entered is a save point; `save_run` and `load_run` are what a game's key and start call.
- The engine's own state captured and restored, including every burning cell and every cell with gas on every map, and the pools, cooldowns and charges of every entity the game saved, so a continued run keeps what its abilities had spent and a cooldown is still live at the clock it was saved on.
- The unload bridge: `Stash`, the last save the game encoded, which `SavePlugin` writes through `Saves` when a browser page is hidden or unloaded and, everywhere, on the frame the app exits. The handler runs outside the app and cannot borrow the world, so the game keeps the stash fresh and clears it when a save must not survive. `Saves` shares its backend by `Arc` so the handler writes through the same one.
- Settings, kept by `SavePlugin`: what a player chose, remembered in a `settings` slot apart from the run and never forgotten with it, recalled before the first frame and written when a setting changes. A plain map of names to words and not the versioned envelope, so it outlives an upgrade, and a name or a choice a build does not know is passed over.
- Records, kept by `SavePlugin` in a game with `RecordsPlugin`: every run's record, kept in a `records` slot, `RECORDS_SLOT`, apart from the run's and never deleted with it, read before the first frame and written whenever the list changes. Every field of a record defaults, so a record an earlier or a later build wrote still reads, and a slot that does not parse at all is warned about and written over by the next record filed.

## Tier 3: rl-engine

- The facade re-exporting every crate.
- `RoguelikePlugins`, the front door: `RoguelikePlugins::new(title, cols, rows)` opens a window sized to the glyph terminal and adds what every game adds, `TerminalPlugin`, `FullscreenPlugin`, `CorePlugin`, `FovPlugin`, `MapViewPlugin`, `UiPlugin` and `CapturePlugin`, with `.cell`, `.font`, `.typeface`, `.map` and `.across` for the rest. No subsystem is in it, and anything in it can be replaced or switched off as in any Bevy plugin group. Every example game and tutorial step starts from it.
- A prelude worth globbing: core, grid, mapgen, world, rules, the Bevy layer, render, UI, overworld and save, in one `use`. It leaves out `Rect`, because Bevy's prelude has one of its own and a game that globs both would have to disambiguate every use; a doc test globs both preludes and names a type from each crate, so the next collision fails there rather than in someone's game. The Bevy layer's part holds what a game writes: the effects a game names only in RON, and the pieces other engine crates build on such as `Bystanders`, `Offered` and `FlowFields`, stay at the crate root.
- `templates/starter`, a `cargo generate` template for a new game: one file with a generated floor of rooms, a carried torch and braziers in the dark, goblins that notice by sight and light and search where they last saw the player, bump-to-attack combat, a status row, a log and a look cursor, and four headless tests. `scripts/check-template.sh` renders it with the engine taken from the checkout and runs its formatting, clippy and tests, and CI runs the script, so the template cannot fall behind the API. The script also refuses a `cargo generate` line in the README or the guide that does not name the release with `--tag`.
- Releases: a tag is `v` and the workspace version, and pushing one publishes its release page from its `CHANGELOG.md` section through `scripts/release-notes.sh`.

## The guide

`docs/guide` is an mdBook that builds a small roguelike, Warren, in nine chapters, each playable in the browser: a map on screen and walking, sight and memory, blows and the log, things to carry and minds, a knack, two floors, content in RON, an action of the game's own, and where to go next.
Each step is a runnable binary in `examples/tutorial/src/bin`, so every chapter's code is compiled by CI and can be played on its own; the chapters quote the sources through mdBook anchors rather than restating them, and `scripts/check-guide.sh` fails the build if an anchor, an image or a table-of-contents entry stops resolving.
The same book's `docs/guide/src/systems` is a reference page per subsystem, thirty of them, for reading once the chapters have taught how the pieces fit rather than while learning them.
Each page names its plugins and the files it documents in a manifest `scripts/check-systems.py` checks against the code, and the check fails the build, not just warns, when a plugin lands with no page or a page falls behind the source it names.

## The fight: Foundry

A commando fights down ten decks of a droid foundry, and the worked example of combat depth: `examples/foundry`.
Blasters run hot and lock until they cool, by a component of Foundry's own, a slug pistol or rifle spends slugs through the engine's `AmmoPlugin` and runs dry, and a heavy repeater fires twice a step and locks as fast; a weapon locked or dry is put by as the engine's `Stowed`, which `Loadout` simply stops finding, and the heat shows on the gear row as Foundry's facet and the slugs left as the engine's.
Trooper and heavy droids fire their own `RangedAttack`, built in rather than held, so `ShootAtRange` fires it and no weapon ever runs hot in a droid's hands; nothing on deck one shoots, and the trooper, from deck two, does so from four tiles, inside the lamp's six; a probe's radar is a `DarkSight` that an ion hit jams. A probe carries no weapon: it keeps a spotted commando three to five tiles off with the engine's `Keep` on its enemies and hangs there with the engine's `Hover`, and on noticing the commando it stands and signals, a work of the game's kind `signalling` for the four turns `alarm` names in `monsters.ron`, which a hit starts over and a kill ends, so the alarm can be stopped; once it finishes, on every turn it takes knowing where the commando is it shouts the alarm, a noise of the game's own loud enough to fill a deck, with a red pulse on the probe when the commando can see it, that brings every droid that hears it to where the probe stood rather than to the commando; the log says when it starts signalling and, once, when the alarm sounds, and a probe that loses the commando signals again before it shouts again. Droids hear a shot ten steps off, so a firefight draws them; the critters, coolant rats, scrap crabs and the lamp moths that swarm the reactor ring, are deaf.
Every gun and every shooting droid names the `look` its shot flies as inside its `ranged` attack in `items.ron` and `monsters.ron`, written as an ability's look is in `abilities.ron`, a red bolt, a blue ion charge or a pale round, and the damage kinds know nothing of it; a `melee` attack takes a `look` the same way, though none is given one yet; a shot is seen to cross the deck and hurts when it arrives, and any key skips it with the hit already taken.
The commando carries a `Stealth` with no skill, which makes it a subject a droid has to notice rather than one seen the instant it comes into view; each droid names its own `notice` in `monsters.ron`, sure of a lit commando within eight tiles and of an unlit one only within two, with a roll a turn past that, and a probe's signalling starts on that noticing.
The decks below the first are dark, and the commando's shoulder lamp is the stealth trade: `L` switches it off, and then, away from the stores' wall lamps, a droid without radar has to be beside you to see you.
A charge set on the reactor console on decks three, six and nine, or on the core on deck ten, is a fact the quest tracker counts, and each charge's pick offers whatever of the three upgrades the run has not already taken.
The run is won at the lift out on deck one, where the commando came in: a lift with no `Transition`, so the engine refuses a `GoThrough` on it and gives the turn back, and Foundry answers the refusal, with a line before the core is charged and, after, the fact that finishes the mission's fifth quest, `victory: true`, which ends the run won.
Damage is kinetic, energy, ion, electricity or thermal, read through one table of resistances in `content.rs` that the damage pipeline applies ahead of armor: a chassis takes half a slug and twice an ion hit, flesh barely feels ion, and electricity, a live cable's, hurts both alike and goes through plate.
A repair drone, from deck three, rebuilds what the commando wrecks: `droids::repair` pushes a `Wrecks` sense of the droid wrecks it can see, its `RepairWrecks` tactic walks there and begins the engine's work, "repairing", for its `repairs` turns plus one for every two points of the wreck's health, and `rebuild_wrecks` answers `WorkDone` by standing the wreck up with the engine's `revive` at half its health. Its row reads `repair drone (repairing)` while it works, and a drone shot at half health or worse breaks off and runs.
Grenades are throwable items with a `land` trigger bursting where each comes down, spent by its one charge: a frag burst, an incendiary that leaves the deck burning through `FirePlugin`, an ion burst that jams every radar in it, and smoke through `GasPlugin` thick enough to hide in, each thrown from the pack, which `t` opens on the first thing that flies, with no system of Foundry's own; each is heard where it lands through the engine's `Noise` effect, a `blast` fourteen steps or smoke's `hiss` six, so droids come to look.
Healing is found, not given: a stim closes a wound at once and a medkit mends two a turn for ten turns, and neither is a system Foundry wrote. Each is an item with a `use` trigger and one charge in `items.ron`, so the engine lands the mend or the `mending` where the commando stands and spends one off the stack for it.
The nanite plate mends its wearer on a worn clock and the cloak plate, used worn, makes them unseen until it comes off.
Every weapon and piece of armor is enchanted by the deck it is found on from `levels.ron`, and an item's `enchant` block in `items.ron` is the one place that says how far and what each level adds: armor and damage by default, and a helmet's dark sight, the nanite plate's clock or the cloak's turns only where written.
A run starts from the engine's title, `TitlePanel`, over Foundry's own picture of the works, painted in `TitleSet::Backdrop` by `title::paint_backdrop`: the furnace breathing, sparks up the chimney and chassis riding the line, with the engine's Continue, New Game, Settings and Exit in the rows under the gantry. The engine holds the run back while it is up, so `run::start` asks nothing about a title, only whether `ContinueRun` asks for `run::resume` instead; the pause and end menus offer Main menu back to it.
`\` opens a cheat menu of Foundry's own, `cheats.rs`, on the engine's modal stack and list widget: reveal the deck, heal, godmode, which is the engine's `Invulnerable`, the lift a deck down or up, and a search of the armory by name that puts what is picked in the pack the way a pickup would, at the level the arrows across set.
What each monster and item is lives in `monsters.ron` and `items.ron`, and where each turns up in `monster_spawns.ron` and `item_spawns.ron`, a row per band, loaded with the engine's `load_spawns` and, for items, its `loot::load`. A monster's line types what the engine plays with its parts, `keep_away` and `drops` among them, and becomes `ActorParts` over Foundry's defaults, the engine's notice and remains on every kind, so `spawn_monster` adds only its look, its profile's resistances, its radar and its alarm.
The armory is Foundry's `ItemMaker`: the engine's `LootPlugin` scatters each deck's floor on the arrival that built it, one item beside every armory mark, two beside every store and `3 + deck` loose, leaves what a kill's kind drops, and fills the crates and lockers, which ask for kinds of thing by tag, slugs, a weapon, armor and a med, so a locker on a deeper deck holds deeper gear.
A deck is grown, not scattered: `assets/layout.ron` holds its shapes, three districts, halls, cells and service passages, and two recipes, wide assembly halls for the upper three decks and a tighter warren with more ways round below, each 84 by 48, and a deck that falls short of its recipe is built again from the next attempt of the same seed. `foundry --deck N --seed S` prints one. Every other room of a deck is furnished by the engine's `StampEachRoom` as what it was for, a machine shop, a charging bay, a parts store, a pump room, a control room, scrap sorting, a loading dock or a maintenance corner, each drawn small and again large: machinery props that are scenery, and floor tiles, hazard stripe, oil stain, scorch mark, drain grate and cable trench, that change nothing underfoot, so a room says what it was and costs the deck nothing it was worth. Its pieces are prefab files in `assets/prefabs/`: two armories, two stores, the reactor chamber and the core, each stamped by name from `Prefabs::piece`, with the lockers and crates in them put down by the engine's `PrefabPlugin` and the consoles' `R` the one `Mark` Foundry fills itself. Decks two, four, five, seven and eight also hold a guard post, a locker and a weapon two decks ahead held by two sentries a deck ahead and a brute two ahead, drawn from `roles.ron`, which names a sentry and a brute role over the droids, through the spawn table at whatever deck the post is stamped on; the roster is Foundry's `ActorMaker`, whose `make_at` is the same `spawn_monster` the deck's own population spawns through, and that population keeps off every slot. A guard fights and searches as any droid does and then walks back to its post. `foundry --prefabs` prints the coverage report of every drawn slot on every deck, and a test holds it free of gaps.
A run is saved on the way out, by the engine's stash when the window closes or the menu quits, by the engine again when the menu leaves the run for the title, and on every deck arrival through `SavePlugin::on_arrival()`; `save.rs` names the commando, droids, items with their heat, and lifts, and the upgrades taken and the deepest deck, and `run::resume` builds the foundry again from the saved seed and restores the run into it. A replay, a recording or a capture saves in memory, so a scripted run neither starts on the player's save nor writes over it. Death and a win delete it, and so does New Game over it, through the engine's `AbandonRun`. The upgrades taken and the deepest deck are the run's, put back with `reset_on_new_run` when it ends, so a run begun after another in the same launch inherits neither.
It is a library with a thin binary, so every system is in one `FoundryPlugin` that the window and the headless tests both add, and `tests/fingerprint.rs` pins a scripted run.

## The game: Undermere

A delve from a lakeside town through a ring of standing stones, down ten floors of a sunken kingdom to the thing that drowned it: `examples/undermere`, the first example written to be a whole game rather than the worked example of one system.
It is three parts in, the skeleton that plays the whole loop thin, the items that make a run's builds, and the monsters that live on its floors; `examples/undermere/DESIGN.md` is the game and the parts still to come.
Mereholt is one hand-drawn piece at dusk, lit by its lamps and windows, and its Drowned Gate leads to floor one and back. The adventurer knows it by heart: the first arrival learns every cell of it, its furniture with it, so the town is drawn whole from the first frame.
Under it each band of floors has its own walls, floors, ambient and fixtures, built from the engine's passes: rooms behind doors in the Upper Halls, caves in the Fungal Deeps, BSP crypts in the Catacombs, rooms with pools of deep water in the Drowned Cathedral, caves with lava in the Forge of Embers, and the Throne, whose piece seats the Drowned King among his court.
Every floor is seen dimly by its own ambient beyond the adventurer's lantern, which dims as they descend, so a floor's shape reads and is remembered while stealth still turns on lit against dim.
A monster is a short core and a list of named traits from `traits.ron`, wits, speed, sight, noticing, hearing, light, resistances, armor, abilities and statuses held for life, folded at load into one definition, with a value two of its traits both set refused naming both; the folded values fill the engine's `ActorParts` over Undermere's own defaults, its `NOTICE` on every kind, and `spawn_monster` adds only its marker, its look, its tricks, the pack and slots of a kind that uses things, and sleep.
An item lends traits to its wearer the same way, through the engine's own seams while it is worn: armor and resistances on the thing for `Loadout` to count, a status the thing holds for `UNTIL_CURED` turns, dark sight, and the thing's own light.
The run is won the moment the Drowned King dies, a quest his death finishes, and however it ends it writes one `RecordRun`: a score of five lines, depth, the slain by the engine's `balance::threat`, treasure, mini-bosses and victory, and the run's numbers, which the end screen and the records the title opens both draw.
It opens on the engine's `TitlePanel` over a painted night, the black lake under stars and a moon, mist on the water, the town's windows and the gate's glow, and a run left for the title is written down for Continue.
Its screen is 160 cells by 45 with square map tiles two narrow cells wide, `RoguelikePlugins::across(2)`, the rail down the right, the log along the bottom and every other screen over the map.
Things are the run's only progression, and most are not known when found: potions, scrolls, staffs, wands and rings wear a face drawn for the run, `Unknown` until a use, a scroll of identify, a ring put on or a scroll asking what to read it on shows what they are, and a worn thing is `unidentified chain mail` until its level is known, by being worn long enough or identified, and then carries its level unless it is nought, `sword` and `+2 chain mail`; the pack and the look cursor say what any thing of its kind is at nought, what is known of its level and how many more turns wearing it will take.
Meanwhile the vitals' armor, the look cursor's forecast and the targeting box's chance to hit count a worn thing not yet known at its kind's plain numbers, in `ViewSet::Annotate` over the engine's true ones, so neither tells a level the wearing has not, and the armor is marked uncertain only while something unknown worn could move it, so an unidentified ring of light leaves it plain.
Scrolls of enchanting, one on every floor at least, raise whatever the player reads them onto through a `UseOn`; heavy weapons and armor want more strength than the adventurer's twelve, costing accuracy and armor for every point short, which the pack and the look cursor say under each such thing, in the bad tone when the adventurer is short; and a thing found below nought is `Stuck`, clinging once worn, its level then said to be below nought, how far not yet known, until it is.
The armory is ten weapons, an axe that sweeps, spears that pierce and a dagger five times as hard on the unaware among them, armor whose weight is loud, a bow spending arrows through the engine's ammunition, staffs and wands zapped from the pack at a cell through `UseItemAt`, rings, eleven potions to drink or throw, and nine scrolls, with the game's own effects, enchanting, identifying, domination, polymorph and the rest, registered beside the engine's.
The bestiary is some thirty kinds across the ten floors, each a short core and its tricks from `traits.ron`, which the look panel says outright for a plain trait and as a question mark for a trick until the adventurer has seen it land; five are mini-bosses, each in one of two lairs drawn a run and never the same twice, worth the score's five hundred, with an entourage of its own kind ready to be led by `Keep::leader` once it falls in beside its chief. Floor ten's boss is drawn from three, the Drowned King wringing a thrall from his pool, the Mother Below spawning from her cave's walls, and the Ember Wyrm's wider breath, and each of the five mini-bosses and the boss carries one of five artifacts, known on sight, with a cost of its own. A floor feels lived in: a share of its monsters sleep, by the engine's new `StatusDef::sleeps`, falling with depth; a goblin war band comes together round its own fire, mostly asleep with its leader keeping watch; a beast stands over a feeding ground; a patrol walks a loop; and a shackled captive, neutral until freed through the engine's new actor `Offers`, fights beside the adventurer once freed, follows it by the engine's new `Keep::leader` and travels the stairs beside it, kept through a save. A monster with the wits uses what it carries, by the engine's new `UseCarried`, a trick answers its own trigger moment, hit, struck or died, and a golem turns a zapped bolt back on whoever cast it, by the engine's new `Reflects`. `UndermerePlugin` adds the engine's gameplay plugins as well as the game's own systems, so the window and the headless tests play one game from one list; `tests/fingerprint.rs` pins a scripted run, and a soak run by hand plays fifty seeds to their ends, won by about one in seven after part 3's tuning, up from one in a hundred and fifty before it.
Its glyphs are drawn in DejaVu Sans Mono, which it ships beside its licence, so its water, trees and gate read the same on every platform.

## Not built yet

- Heat and cold that put fire out, liquids that flow, and wind.
- Lit detection ranges, a light-averse tactic, and a ranged penalty in the dark once accuracy exists.
- Scripted encounters, which want an ability's effect list without the turn, the cost and the cursor.
- Bevy UI presenters over the panel views (phase H of `docs/design/ui.md`). The views and collectors already do not know which backend draws them; what a node tree would add is wrapping, proportional text, mouse hover and sub-cell bars. Deferred until a game asks for one of those, since it is a second set of presenters to keep and its tests are node trees rather than the exact-text ones that have caught the bugs so far.
- Hover, tooltips and click-to-travel, which `Pointer` now makes possible and nothing yet does.
- Instanced terminal rendering; one sprite per cell is the known scaling limit.
- Props that block sight, and a prop that blocks the way without stopping a shot: a prop's `blocks` is movement and shots together, and none blocks sight.
- Furnishing that works on any map: a weighted pool with bands and a budget, rules that dress a room of any size rather than centre a fixed piece, and a way to find room-like areas on a cave so the room passes reach it. `docs/TODO.md` has all three.
