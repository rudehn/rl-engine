# Changelog

A release is a tag, `v` and the workspace version.
The starter template pins the release it was written against, and a game made from it moves to a newer one by changing that tag and reading here what moved.
Pushing a tag publishes its release page from its section here, through `scripts/release-notes.sh`.

## Unreleased

- A used thing is offered and described in the bag. `ItemRow::used` is what its effects do, in their own words through `Effects::describe`, and `ItemRow::usable` is what the use key and the footer ask instead of "does it lend an ability", so a thing carrying `OnUse` is usable from the sea chest or the pack and says what it would do. The `use:` line reads the same for either carrier, because both ask the same list.
- Corsair: a pull of rum is what the bottle does, not an ability the captain knows. The `swig` leaves `abilities.ron` and its four effects move onto the bottle as `on_use` in `items.ron`, with `Consumable` spending one for a drink, which is what `Charge(1)` had been doing from the ability's side. The captain now knows three things rather than four and the keys are `1` to `3`; a bottle is drunk with the bag's own use key. Corsair is the second game on the seam, and moving it turned up that its test harness adds plugins separately from its binary, which `docs/TODO.md` already tracks: the drink test failed until `ConsumablesPlugin` was added to both.
- The inspect forecast fights at a distance. `rl_rules::forecast::Arms` holds both of what an actor can do with a turn, the melee rolls and the shot, and `Arms::at` picks between them by the rule `resolve_attacks` uses: adjacent is the melee weapon, further is the shot if it reaches, and nothing at all beyond that. `Combatant::armed` is the constructor that takes it, `Loadout::shots` and `Loadout::arms` fill it in `rl-bevy`, and the inspect view passes the pair's own distance. Before this the forecast counted only melee rolls, so a droid carrying nothing but a gun read as unable to hurt anything and the panel called it "easy" from across a room it could shoot you across; and a brawler read as dangerous from a distance it could not reach. Both now read what they can really do at the distance they are at.

- Fixed: a mind could decide, turn after turn, on a step the move resolver refuses. `Hunt`, `SearchLastKnown`, `Follow`, `FleeWhenHurt`, `GiveWay`, `Wander` and half of `Shadow` checked a straight-line fallback with `can_step` alone, which says the cell is open and nothing about the way in; the resolver's `corner_ok` refuses a diagonal squeezing between two cells the actor cannot stand on. The step was refused silently, so the mind decided the same way on its next turn, and a droid could shuffle at a doorway for as long as its target stood still. Every tactic that picks a neighbour itself now asks `steps_to`, which is `can_step` and the corner rule together. It is a hair stricter than the resolver, which reads terrain alone where `can_step` also refuses an occupied or burning cell: declining a legal step costs one turn, and deciding on an illegal one costs every turn until the world moves. Foundry's fingerprint is re-baselined for it.
- Three performance items investigated and all three closed without a change, which is the point of having benches. `one_player_turn_in_smoke` in `crates/rl-bevy/benches/turns.rs` and `cell_churn` in `crates/rl-ui/benches/frame.rs` are what settled the last two, and they stay so the next argument starts from a number.
  - A lit frame's field of view is already about as cheap as it can be. Counters said there is no double cast (exactly 1.00 shadowcasts per actor per turn, all in the frame's pass, `sense` firing zero times) and that a mind asks its viewshed thirty-two point-visibility questions a turn, which the 10.3-microsecond shadowcast answers at 0.32 each. Point line-of-sight would lose by sixty-four actors, since the shadowcast is flat in the crowd and the question count is not.
  - The veil's global opacity epoch is free. Thick gas that veils costs less per turn than thick gas that does not (373 against 387 microseconds at thirty-two actors), because every viewshed is already recast every turn and marking them all stale adds no casts.
  - An instanced terminal would buy nothing. Over four thousand cells, a still frame changes none, a frame the player stepped in changes about fifty, and a flickering torch churns 293 a frame of which zero are a new glyph. `flush_terminal` only rewrites a `Text2d` when the character changed, so the worst lit frame is about fifty glyph rewrites and three hundred colour writes, not four thousand text re-layouts.

- The `Turn` schedule runs on one thread. It is dozens of small systems and it runs once per actor turn rather than once per frame, so Bevy's multi-threaded executor was paying a per-system handoff tens of thousands of times a second for systems none of which is worth a thread. Profiled by the new `crates/rl-bevy/benches/passes.rs`, which strips the engine back rather than sampling it: a pass of the fifty-two systems a fighting game adds cost 91 microseconds, and a build whose actors carried no `Mind` cost the same 91, so the deciding was free and the dispatch was everything. A pass now costs 4.3 microseconds, and one player turn with a hundred and twenty-eight awake minds 1.9 milliseconds rather than 14.4. A game with a genuinely heavy system of its own in the pass can put the multi-threaded executor back with the same one-line call.
- `crates/rl-bevy/benches/passes.rs`: where a pass goes, by how much engine is in the app, and where a sight recast goes, split into the shadowcast and the light gate. The second says the recast is 10.3 microseconds of shadowcast against 1.3 of gate, so the lit frame's cost wants fewer recasts rather than a cheaper gate; `docs/TODO.md` says what stands in the way of that.

- `OnUse` and `Consumable`, through `ConsumablesPlugin`: what a thing in the bag does when it is used, landed on whoever used it where they stand, and what the use spends the thing. A use is not an ability, and saying so is the point. An item that is aimed or waits on a cooldown still lends one with `Grants`; an item that is simply used carries its own effects, never enters the ability registry and never appears in `Known`, and what it costs the thing is written on the thing rather than as a `Charge(1)` cost of an ability. `docs/design/items.md` is new and says where the line falls and why, along with the alternatives it rejects, `on_equip` among them.
- `Effects`, the engine's one built list of effects: built from `EffectSpec` against the registered kinds, reporting every spec that would not build, landed onto an ability's full footprint or onto one cell with `land_on`, and described with `describe`. `ability.rs` and `props.rs` had a private copy each, the second already differing from the first in how it reported an unregistered name; both now carry this, and a used item is the third carrier rather than the third copy. Every effect in the engine still rolls its chance from `AbilityRng`, whatever lands it, so no subsystem shifts another's dice.
- `EffectSpec` deserializes itself, so a game's own content file can carry a list of effects: `(kind: "Harm", chance: 50, args: (..))`, chance certain unless given, arguments left as text for whichever layer registered the effect. Each engine file had mirrored the authored form privately, which meant a game that wanted an effect list had to write that mirror a third time.
- Foundry: two ways to heal, and a run that no longer depends on drawing the Stims upgrade to have any. A stim closes seven to thirteen at once; a medkit is worth twice that and pays it two a turn over ten turns, which is ten turns of the deck to live through first, and a second kit starts the ten again rather than mending twice as fast. Both are found loose, a stim in a supply crate and a medkit in an armory locker, and both are spent by being used. Each says what it does on itself: `items.ron` grew `on_use` and `uses`, the effects are built once per definition and shared by every copy through an `Arc`, and `gear::Armory::load` takes the `Abilities` and the `EffectKinds` it resolves them against. `abilities.ron` is back to one entry, the upgrade's own `stims`, and carrying a stim teaches the commando nothing.
- A light that moves marks stale only the viewsheds it could have changed, instead of every viewshed in the world. A viewshed is cut down to what is lit inside its own range, so a lamp at the far end of the window cannot change what a mind twenty tiles away sees; `update_lighting` now takes the discs of the emitters that appeared, vanished or moved and marks the viewers those discs can reach. `Rect::union` is new in `rl-core` for it. Worth 10 per cent of a lit frame with the crowd around the player and nothing once it is spread out, which is smaller than the reading suggested and is recorded as such in `docs/TODO.md`: the cost of a lit frame is the re-gate itself, not who is re-gated.
- `crates/rl-ui/benches/frame.rs`, the frame's own bench, as `rl-bevy`'s is the turn's: a still frame, a frame in which a carried lamp moved, and the same by how many panels are up, each at eight, thirty-two and sixty-four actors in sight. It settled three items of `docs/TODO.md` on the day it was written, two of them against the reading that filed them.

- `DamageStages` defaults to `SubtractArmor` rather than to nothing. Every game in this repository and the starter template inserted exactly `DamageStages(vec![Box::new(SubtractArmor)])`, and a game that forgot it got raw damage, armor ignored, with no word about why. A game that wants a pipeline of its own inserts one and this default is never built; a game that really wants raw damage inserts `DamageStages(Vec::new())` and says so. The explicit line in every game and the guide still works and is still worth reading, but it is no longer load-bearing.
- `app.reset_on_new_run::<R>()` and `reset_on_new_run_with::<R>(make)`: a plugin declares the resource it keeps of a run where it creates it, and the engine puts it back when the run ends. `clear_run` had named `Fire`, `Gases`, `Offered`, three airborne queues and `Lighting` by hand, so a subsystem that kept something of a run had to go and edit `CorePlugin` or leak it into the next run. What `CorePlugin` owns itself, the clock, the occupancy index and what has acted, is still put back directly: its absence would be a broken world rather than a plugin nobody added.
- One airborne queue instead of three. `cue::Airborne<L>`, the `Lands` marker and `app.add_airborne::<L>()` replace `AirborneShots`, `AirborneThrows` and abilities' `Airborne`, which held the same five lines that stash a landing and tell the hold it launched, and the same four that take the stash, land it, free the hold and mark the pass. Each subsystem keeps its own `land`, with its own parameters; what it no longer keeps is the bookkeeping. `Turns::made_progress` is public for the same reason: a landing is work a pass did.
- A key held down skips a cue the way a key pressed does. `skip_on_key` reads `just_pressed`, which a repeat is not, so walking with a direction key held waited out every cue in sight: measured in Foundry, two seconds of a held key walked 15 steps on an empty lane and 4 beside a probe that pulses on each of its turns. `TurnHold::ask_skip` and `take_skip_asked` are the seam, since the repeat is paced in `rl-ui` and the skipping is done in `rl-render`, which is below it.
- `ViewSet::Sight`, a phase before `ViewSet::Collect`, and the `Sighted` resource it fills. What the player can see is a full entity scan, two allocations and a sort, and `browse`, both cursors, the nearby collector, the inspect collector and the target view each asked for it separately: up to six times a frame. It is collected once before the keys are read and once before the views are built, which are the two moments the answer can differ, and every reader reads the same list.
- `CombatRng` and `AbilityRng` are out of `rl_bevy::prelude`, where they had let a game reach for an engine subsystem's stream. They are still at the crate root for the crates that build on them. A game's own draws come from `Seed::stream`, kept in a resource of its own: Corsair's loot drops and its on-hit statuses each have one now, `items::Drops` and `statuses::Inflicts`, where both had been rolling from the combat stream and shifting the dice of blows that had not been struck yet.
- `crates/rl-bevy/benches/turns.rs`, the first bench above tier 1: one player turn as the crowd of awake minds grows, and again with the floor loaded with items nobody will pick up. One player turn costs 0.40 ms with one awake mind, 1.12 at eight, 3.63 at thirty-two, 6.96 at sixty-four and 14.39 at a hundred and twenty-eight, which is linear at about 110 microseconds per awake mind. Two thousand items on the floor add 0.39 ms to a whole turn with the crowd held at sixteen, so the perceive stage's full-world scans, which `docs/TODO.md` had ranked as the engine's next ceiling, are not one.

- Foundry's run is ten decks now, not three: `decks::DECKS` is 10, and the reactor chamber that used to stamp only deck three now stamps deck three, six and nine, with a fourth charge on a new core prefab on deck ten, the same five-by-five chamber with machinery down both walls instead of one.
  The line droid, probe droid, heavy droid and coolant rat's spawn bands in `monsters.ron` extend to cover decks four through ten, so every deck through ten has something to spawn.
  `lifts::deck_line` names every deck from one to ten instead of falling to the bare "Deck {n}." for the seven in between.
  `DESIGN.md`'s progression table now says all ten decks exist and only the four zones remain planned.

  Ranged weapons are the strongest thing in the game and in the dark the lamp's six tiles is the whole horizon, so both halves of the ramp are handed out a deck at a time.
  Nothing on deck one shoots: the line droid has no bolt any more and strikes with the clamp it runs the lines with, which suits a worker droid, and the probe still carries no attack at all.

- The documentation is reachable from the web, not only from an editor. `docs/README.md` is the index GitHub renders when someone opens the folder, listing the guide, the inventory, the nine design docs, the plan, the reviews and the outstanding work.
  The README's documentation section links them instead of naming them in backticks, and adds `docs/design/` and `docs/TODO.md`, which it had never mentioned.
  Only `docs/guide` is published as a website, so the guide's last chapter now points at the overview, the plan, the rules and all nine design docs with absolute links that resolve from the published book as well as from GitHub; they were backticks, which on the site were dead ends.

- The README's feature list catches up with the engine: items and throwing, abilities and targeting, stealth, noise and hearing, fire and gas, props and remains, the view-collector-presenter split behind the panels, and replay and the morgue.
- The controls screen lists root-level keys only: what works while the world is in front of the player, and the key that opens each screen.
  A key that only means something while a screen is up is that screen's own and is written along its bottom border, where it already was, so the bag no longer declares wear, drop, use and throw and the log no longer declares scrolling and filtering.
  Two screens may now default to the same key without clashing, since a modal's keys are read only while it is the top screen.
  `EngineKey` loses `Wear`, `Drop`, `UseCarried`, `ThrowCarried`, `ScrollLog` and `FilterLog`, which described screens' own keys; what is left is the cursors and the key that opens each screen.
- The noise reading fades over the turns after a noise rather than snapping to nothing: a probe's klaxon filled the bar, emptied it the next turn and filled it again, which read as a fault rather than as a loud room. `NoiseFade` is what is left of a reading one turn later, six tenths by default, and whatever arrives in a turn outranks what is left of the last one. A klaxon takes about ten turns to die away; a shot, four.
  Foundry's gauge is scaled to three thousand rather than a thousand, so a shot next door reads about a third of it and a klaxon, eight times louder, pegs it. Scaled to the shot alone, everything pegged and the bar said only that something had happened.
- `app.reads::<M>()`, the engine's answer to a plugin that reads a message another plugin writes. Bevy refuses a reader for a message nobody registered, so a system that reads what an optional plugin writes panicked in every game that left that plugin out; five plugins had each worked around it with a bare `add_message` and a paragraph of explanation. `Reads` is the opposite of `Needs`: one says what a plugin cannot work without and fails loudly, the other says what it can work without and means it.
- Fixed: the noise reading was only drawn on a one-row vitals strip, so a game with a taller panel, which is Foundry, never saw it. It is a line of its own there now.
- Fixed: a key held when the window lost focus stayed held, because the release happened somewhere else and the window never saw it: after a screenshot, keys stopped registering until the key was pressed and released again. Focus changing either way now forgets what was down, and the hold with it.
- Fixed: a prop could still seal a room off. Counting a cell's walkable neighbours was never the right question: the cell below a two-wide store has three floor neighbours and is still the one way in, because the diagonals either side of it cut a corner the move resolver refuses. Foundry now floods the deck as a walker walks it, corner rule included, and a crate that would shut anything away goes somewhere else. The property test floods the same way, which is what it should have done the first time.
- A noise reading on the vitals strip: `VitalsView::noise` is how loud it is where the player stands, the loudest noise that reached them this turn and zero on a quiet one, held for the turn rather than the frame so a reading nobody could see never flashes past.
  `NoiseHeard` carries `left`, how much loudness was still on it when it arrived, which is the number the gauge reads and the number `Hearing::threshold` was already measured against.
  `VitalsPanel::noise(name, loudest)` names the gauge and says how loud a full one is; an empty name leaves it out. Foundry's commando has ears now, and a shot at the next tile fills the gauge.
- A nearby row says what something is doing about you in words rather than by a mark: `Row::alert` is `Alert::{Unaware, Searching, Hunting}`, read off stealth and hearing, and the panel writes it after the name as `(hunting)`. `Row::aware` and `Row::heard` are gone, and so are the `!` and `?` marks.
  What each state is called is the panel's, through `NearbyPanel::alerts(AlertWords::new(..))`, since one game's monsters sleep where another's stand idle; Foundry's droids are idle, searching and hunting.
- A nearby row is its own health bar: health washes the row's background from the left in the health's tone rather than taking a column of its own, so a hurt thing reads at a glance. `NearbyLayout::bar_width` and `NearbyPanel::bars` are gone.
- Fixed: remains went on watching. A watcher is anything carrying `Notice` that is not `Dead`, and remains are deliberately not `Dead`, so a body kept noticing the player: a deck with every droid dead still read as one the commando was seen on. `WasLiving` now takes `Notice`, `Aware`, `Hearing` and `Heard` off a body as well, so what is dead notices nothing and hears nothing.
- Fixed: a line about a blow was worded with what its subject is called when the line is spoken rather than when the blow landed, so a killing blow read "You hit the coolant rat remains for 2" and "You kill the coolant rat remains!".
  A row is made inside the pass and spoken after it, and the dead are renamed in between; a row now keeps what its subjects were called when it was made, and falls back to reading them now for a line told outside the turns.
- Foundry: an armory locker wants a keycard, and spends it. Keycards are content like anything else: an item tagged `keycard`, found loose and dropped by line and heavy droids, and `props::spend_the_keycard` takes one off the bag when a locker opens, so a card found is a locker opened and no more.
- `CursorStyle`: how a screen marks the cell it points at, as one parametrized enum. `Glow { tone, pulse }` washes the cell and leaves what stands there showing; `Ticks { marks, tone, pulse }` frames it with four marks and never covers it. Both take a `ToneId`, and either may breathe toward a second tone or sit still.
  `InspectPanel`, `NearbyPanel` and `TargetPanel` take one in their constructor through `.cursor(..)`, and `cursor::mark` is the one drawing, so looking, aiming and tabbing through what is in sight cannot drift apart.
  `InspectLayout::pointers` is gone: it is `InspectLayout::cursor` now, a whole style rather than four characters.
- A prop under the look cursor is named and its health shown, and no duel is forecast for it: a crate has health so it can be broken, not so a panel can say how many blows it needs and how long it would take to kill you back.
- A bump into a prop that offers one thing it refuses now reports itself rather than coming to nothing silently, so the offers screen can say what it wants: walking into a locked thing used to do nothing at all.
- Foundry: one mark for every cell a screen points at, the pulsing ticks, so the rail's tab navigation, the look cursor and the targeting cursor read as one thing.
  A crate never stands in a doorway or beside one, which used to seal a room and everything in it off for the run; a blocking prop wants floor in the body of a room, and loose cable, which is walked over, may lie anywhere. A property test over six seeds and three decks floods the deck and fails if a prop cut off a single cell it could reach before: forced back to the old rule it reports 309 cells lost on seed three.
  A deck's loot lands beside its armory and store marks rather than on them, since the marks now hold a locker and a crate.

- Fixed: `DecideSet::Sense` was never configured, so `minds::sense` ran unordered against the whole pass and a mind could decide on sight that had not been recast. It is at the head of the `TurnSet::Decide` chain, where its own doc always said it was.
  Found by Foundry's ambiguity test once a prop held a `Viewshed` in the same pass. Delve's `a_rat_round_a_corner_hears_a_fight_and_comes_to_it` was passing on the old accidental order: its post was chosen out of the player's sight range rather than out of any line to the fight, and it asserted on a trail the rat had rightly dropped by the time it read it. Both are fixed in the test, and `rl-bevy`'s fingerprint tripwire is re-baselined, since minds now decide on sight that was recast first.
- Foundry: a droid's wreck is drawn as `%` from its own definition rather than keeping the glyph of the droid that walked, and a deck's loot lands beside its armory and store marks rather than on them, since those marks now hold a locker and a crate and an item under a crate is an item nothing can pick up.
  Its fingerprint tripwire is re-baselined for both.
- Props, opt-in by adding `PropsPlugin`: a crate, a lever, a plate or a console is content in a `props.ron` rather than a marker component, an action, a resolver and a key in every game that wants one.
  A prop is a cell, a name and a look; whether it blocks, what breaks it, what it offers, what it holds and how hard it is to spot are each optional and separate.
  `Verbs` interns what an interaction is called, `OfferedHere` says what whoever holds the turn could do with what it can reach and why not, `Interact` is the one action, and `Interacted` is how a game answers a verb of its own: no trait to implement and no registry of behaviours, because a game already knows how to answer a message.
  A bump into a prop that offers one thing takes it up, the way a bump into a door opens it.
  A container is `Inventory` on a prop: the engine rolls what it holds and asks the game to spawn it with `FillContainer`, `Take` moves things into the bag and merges stacks the way the ground does, and a crate that can show it is done stops offering and is redrawn as an opened one.
  `ContainerPanel` is the screen, opened by the engine when a container is opened and closed when the player walks away from it; taking does not close it, since opening already cost what the definition said.
  A trap is data: `trigger: (on: Entered | Destroyed, fires: n, effects: [...])` lands the engine's own effects on whoever set it off, counted in `Fired` on the prop, and a game answers `Triggered` only for what no effect can say. `hidden: (spot: n)` keeps a prop out of the map and out of what is listed in sight until a per-turn roll finds it, and a hidden trap still springs. A prop with `health` breaks like anything else.
  `Landing::ability` is now an `Option<AbilityId>`, since a trap lands the same effects an ability does and has none; `EffectKinds::build` is public so anything holding effects as content can build them; and `add_stream` no longer adds a second deriving system when two plugins want the same stream.
  Props are saved by the engine rather than by each game: `SavePlugin` registers the kind, and a prop comes back by definition name with its own history (fired, stocked, emptied, still hidden), where it stood and holding what it held.
  What dies and stays is now a prop too: `Snapshot::remains` became `Snapshot::props` as `PropView`, filled by one contributor, so a mind that walks to wrecks and one that walks to crates read one list; and `RemainsNaming` names a body from one template, `"{what} remains"` by default, applied when it dies and again when a save is continued.
  `rl-ui` gains `InteractKey`, the key that does what is here: it takes the one offer in reach, and where two are offered it takes none, since choosing between them is a question for a screen.
  `PropSet::{Stock, Fill}` orders the engine's ask for what a container holds against a game's answer, so what goes into a crate lands in the frame the crate was put down rather than in that frame or the next.
  Foundry uses all of it: supply crates and armory lockers on every deck, loose live cable that is hidden until it is spotted and harms whoever walks it, wrecks that are named "line droid remains" and can be stripped, and a reactor console that is now a line of RON plus one system reading `Interacted`, in place of an action, a resolver, a key and a refusal message.
  Foundry's fingerprint tripwire is re-baselined: crates, lockers and cable now stand on its decks.
  Foundry's deck-building systems are one chain, since three systems that all spawn, left unordered, hand their commands in whatever order they finish in.
  `docs/design/props.md` is the design and what building it taught.
- Remains, opt-in by adding `RemainsPlugin` and, per actor, spawning it with `LeavesRemains`: what dies stays where it fell for a game to say what that means.
  The remains are the dead actor itself, kept rather than despawned, so nothing of what died is copied or guessed at: whatever the game spawned it with is still on the same entity, under the same save kind the game already registered.
  The engine takes off only `WasLiving`, what it put on and what means alive and acting, so a body holds no turn, blocks no doorway, thinks nothing and cannot be killed twice.
  `RemainsLeft` for a game to answer in `TurnSet::React`, `Snapshot::remains` as `RemainsView { id, pos, side }` so a mind can walk to one, and the save lays them back down on a continued run.
  Whose they were is the only thing the engine says about them: no name, no rot timer, no loot, and nothing about whether they can be searched, stripped or rebuilt. `docs/design/remains.md` is the reasoning.
- `rl-rules` gains `Shadow { keep_within, no_closer_than }`, the enemy-facing twin of `Follow`: it closes on the nearest enemy past `keep_within`, backs off inside `no_closer_than`, and leaves the band between to the next tactic, never lengthening the gap while closing nor shortening it while backing off.

## 0.1.0

The first release.

- The engine's crates in four tiers: `rl-core`; `rl-grid`, `rl-mapgen`, `rl-world` and `rl-rules`, which never depend on Bevy; `rl-bevy`, `rl-render`, `rl-ui`, `rl-overworld` and `rl-save`; and `rl-engine`, the facade a game depends on.
- `RoguelikePlugins`, what every game adds, and a plugin per subsystem: combat, minds, statuses, items, abilities, lighting, stealth, streaming, facts.
- `Seed`, the one thing about randomness a game supplies; every subsystem derives its stream from it.
- `Registries`, every registry the engine reads, filled once; `Names` and `NameRef<T>`, so a game's own content names content and holds ids.
- `CombatRules::new(&sides).hostile(a, b)`, and `hunts` and `allied`.
- Panels as views, collectors and presenters, over `UiPlugin`, which owns the message log, with `add_tone` and `add_modal`.
- `templates/starter`, a game to generate with `cargo generate`.
- The guide in `docs/guide`, and three examples: the tutorial, the delve and Corsair.
