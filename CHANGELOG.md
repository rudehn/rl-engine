# Changelog

A release is a tag, `v` and the workspace version.
The starter template pins the release it was written against, and a game made from it moves to a newer one by changing that tag and reading here what moved.
Pushing a tag publishes its release page from its section here, through `scripts/release-notes.sh`.

## Unreleased

- An actor can be hard for the player to see: one carrying the new `Concealed` component is not drawn on the map, listed in the nearby rail, stepped to by the look and aim cursors or named by the look panel while it stands outside the lit band and is not beside the player.
  Stealth hid a subject only from minds, and the player is no mind, so a monster meant to be hard to see in the dark was drawn plainly.
  What the player sees of a thing is now one question, the new `PlayerSight::shows(at, on, concealed)`, asked by the map, by `InSight` and so `Sighted`, and by the look panel, with the rule itself the new `conceals(lighting, from, at)`; with no `Lighting` every cell is lit and the marker hides nothing.
  The look panel now names only what the map draws: a thing out of the player's sight, or a prop nobody has spotted, under the cursor reads as its ground alone, where it was named and forecast.
  A game adds and removes `Concealed` itself, and the engine does not save it.

- A body keeps neither what the actor offered nor what it answered: `Offers` and `Triggers` are now in `WasLiving`, so they come off as it is laid down and stay on its twin for a revival.
  A captive's offer was still taken up on its corpse, and a body still carrying the actor's own triggers was never armed with those of a prop kind a game dressed it as.
  A game that wants a body to offer something, or to answer a moment, puts that on it at `RemainsLeft`.

- A mind no longer casts an ability aimed at itself alone that would find nothing to act on, where `UseAbility` cast one whenever a foe was in sight: a mend at full health, or a game's raising with no body in reach.
  The new `Effect::would_act(landing, world)` says whether an effect, landed now, would act on anything, read from the world without changing it; it is true unless the effect says otherwise, and the engine's `Mend` says so only with someone hurt among its targets.
  `AbilitiesPlugin`'s new `weigh_self_abilities`, in `DecideSet::Perceive` after `PerceiveSet::Annotate`, takes from what the mind holding the turn may use every self-aimed ability none of whose effects would act on the landing the resolver would land; an ability with no effects is kept, since a game answers it itself.
  What the player is offered is unchanged.
  A game whose monsters know a self-aimed mend now see them keep it for when they are hurt; one with an effect that needs something there to act on implements `would_act` to say so.

- The narrator says whom an ability was used on only when it is aimed at someone: one aimed at the ground that caught one reads `The necromancer uses blink.` and `You use burst.`, where a step through the air pointed past the player read `The necromancer uses blink on you.`
  One aimed at the ground that caught several still reads `You use burst, catching 2.`, and whoever it caught is told by the harm it did them, as before.

- The nearby rail clips a long name and keeps what the thing is doing whole after it, `Grubnak the… (unaware)` where it read `Grubnak the goblin king (unawar…`; a row too narrow to keep four of the name's characters beside the state is clipped from the end as before.

- A mind that opens doors no longer plans a diagonal past a closed door's corner, which the move resolver refuses, so it is no longer held refused at the same step every turn: the new `WorldMap::lets_diagonal_past(p)` is the one corner rule, read by the move resolver and answered by `opening_view`, which prices a closed door at its opening and still lets no diagonal past it.
  The rule reaches the searches through the new `CostSource::lets_diagonal_past(p)`, which A* and `DijkstraMap` ask of both cells a diagonal squeezes between, and which is `is_passable(p)` unless a source says otherwise, so every other source paths as it did.
  To change: a game that wraps `opening_view` in a `CostSource` of its own forwards `lets_diagonal_past` to it, or its wrapper keeps the old disagreement.

- A use that a `Reflects` body turns back, and that leaves nobody else caught where it was aimed, lands whole on its user: its cells become the shape drawn on the user's own cell, the user's cell for a bolt and a burst round them for a ball, where only the targets were swapped and a turned-back bolt still set the reflecting body's cell alight.
  A reflecting body in a burst that caught others still turns back only its own share: the others are hit where it was aimed and the ground stays there, the body is spared, and the user takes its share.
  To change: `Bystanders::reflect(landing)` is `Bystanders::reflect(landing, mode, map)`, taking the shape it was aimed with and the map it bursts on.

- `EffectWorld` says whether a cell is a transition: the new `EffectWorld::is_transition(p)` reads the engine's own `Transition` component at `p`, read by no effect of the engine's own, since `is_free` is silent on the question and a move onto a floor's own way through is the game's to allow or refuse.
  A game effect that moves someone through open ground, a blink or a far teleport among them, checks it beside `is_free` to keep a monster's own trick from landing it exactly on a stair.

- A mind can follow one leader: `Keep::leader(keep_within, no_closer_than)`, the new `Station::Leader`, keeps within its distances of the actor named by the follower's new `Leader(Entity)` component rather than of its nearest ally, with the same steps that never undo what they are doing, and keeps station on its allies as `Keep::allies` does while the leader is dead, on another map or gone, so a band whose chief falls keeps together.
  `MindsPlugin`'s new `sense_leaders` tells it where its leader stands through the new `Led { at, in_sight }` sense, seen or not, since a follower told only of a leader in sight would lose it at the first corner.
  The leader lives on the follower and not in its brain, so one shared brain serves a kind led or not; `rl-save` keeps it across a continued run, by the entity map, when the leader was saved too.
  A game that matches on `Station` exhaustively gains an arm; a brain with `Keep::leader` counts as keeping with its allies for who follows whom through a way.

- The look panel wraps what a game says about a thing: each of `InspectView::facets` is broken onto as many lines as it takes, on spaces, where it was cut to one line with an ellipsis, so a monster's five traits read as sentences.
  A facet is drawn whole or not at all, and those the rectangle has no room for are counted on its last row in the muted tone, `and 2 more` by default and the game's own words with the new `InspectPanel::more("{n} more")`.
  A game whose facets were short sees no change; one whose facets ran long may want a taller panel.

- An actor can offer verbs, as a prop does: the new `Offers` component, a list of `Offering { verb, time, needs }` built with `Offering::new(verb, time)` and `.needing(tag)`, is what an actor offers whoever stands beside it, a captive to free or a person to talk to.
  `offer_here` adds those offers to `OfferedHere` for the one holding the turn, read where the actor stands when the gate runs and never offered to its own holder nor to anyone the actor is hostile to by `CombatRules`, so no key takes up a foe's offer peacefully, and they are refused, resolved and answered with `Interacted` exactly as a prop's offer with no effects is; what taking one up does is the game's, read off `Interacted` by verb, and a verb that takes several turns is answered by beginning work on the target with `Works::begin`.
  `Offers` is read on whatever carries it, a prop too, and is not saved by the engine: the game puts it back on with whatever it saves the actor as.
  A bump into someone who is neither foe nor ally and offers one thing takes it up; one offering more, or one it refuses, comes to nothing as a prop's does and is reported as a `Bumped` with the new `offers` field set, which is what `OffersPanel` now opens on instead of asking whether the thing bumped was a prop.
  A foe is struck and an ally bumped as an ally whatever either offers.
  The offers screen names a person by their `Name` as the look panel lists it: `free Mirela`.
  To change: `Offer::prop`, `Interact::prop`, `Interacted::prop` and `OfferRow::prop` are now `target`, since what offers is no longer always a prop; a `Bumped` built field by field gains `offers`.

- An actor answers its own moments: an actor carrying `Triggers` answers `hit` when its own blow or shot lands (a bite that poisons), the new `struck` when one lands on it (an aura that burns whoever struck it), and the new `died` when it dies (a burst of spores where it fell).
  Whatever an actor answers lands as its own doing, credited to it, without `LandsAsItself`, and its area spares the actor it belongs to: a `struck` aura written `Burst(radius: 1)` lands on everyone round its bearer and not on the bearer.
  A prop's and a thing's triggers still land on everyone in their area.
  One blow that lands reports, in this order and each once, the worn weapon's `hit`, the attacker's own `hit`, and the struck one's `struck`, all at the struck one's cell; a shaped attack reports them once for each foe it strikes, and a miss reports none.
  `struck` is reported as the blow lands, before its damage, so a blow that kills sets off `struck` and then, a pass later, `died`.
  Only an attack, a blow in reach or a shot, strikes: a thrown thing, an ability, a trap or a fire sets off no `struck`.
  `died` is relayed through a `Remnant` as a prop's `destroyed` is, by `report_died` in `TurnSet::React`, which `EffectsPlugin` adds: it lands once, where the actor died, after its body is out of the world, set off `by` whoever killed it.
  The remnant carries the actor's `Name` and `LandsAsItself`, so it stands in for the actor: a foe its burst kills is credited to it, the log says `The myconid hits you for 3.`, and a game that names what killed its player reads the actor's name off the credit.
  A remnant that has landed now keeps its name until the end of the frame, when the new `bury_remnants` despawns it in `EndOfFrame::Bury`, so what reads who did it later in the pass still finds it; a broken prop's burst is named by the prop the same way.
  The narrator places a remnant where its moment happened, so the one it stands in for is seen and named there.
  What a remnant takes from what it stands in for is `RemnantKeeps`, the `Name` by default, and a plugin adds to it with `app.keep_on_remnant::<C>()`; `NarratorPlugin` adds the `Glyph`, so the log colours a dead actor's or a broken prop's name as it colours a living one's.
  A shooter that answers `hit` and is gone before its watched shot lands still lands it, from a remnant.
  To change: nothing for an actor with no `Triggers`; a game that registered moments of its own sees their ids move up by two, since `Moments::STRUCK` and `Moments::DIED` are built in after `pulse`.
- A status can sleep: `StatusDef::sleeps`, written `sleeps: true` in a status file, holds its holder's turns as `holds` does and ends the moment the holder is woken, by harm landing on it, by a noise that reaches it with its `Hearing` threshold left, or by a foe it notices within `SLEEPER_REACH`, two cells.
  A blow on a sleeper is a blow on the unaware: `Watchers::unaware` answers yes for a sleeper whoever strikes it, with or without stealth, so the blow lands at `CombatRules::unaware` or its own percent, and only then wakes it.
  A sleeper notices only within that reach and there by its chance alone, never by its certain radius, so a hider can stand over it; a foe that does not hide, or any foe of a sleeper that sees on sight, wakes it by being in its sight there, and a sleeper watches nobody.
  `StatusPlugin` wakes a sleeper on harm with `wake_on_harm` in `TurnSet::React` and on sight with `wake_on_sight` in `DecideSet::Notice`, and `NoisePlugin` on noise with `wake_on_noise` in `TurnSet::Listen`; each cures the sleep at once and writes `StatusEvent::Cured`.
  `Sleepers` answers who is asleep, and `Statuses::sleeping` and `Statuses::wake` are the rules' half.
  To change: a `StatusDef` written out field by field gains `sleeps: false`; a status with no `sleeps` behaves as it did.
- A mind uses what it carries, when its kind has the wits to: `Wits::USES`, written `"uses"` in content and in the sapient preset, lets the new `UseCarried` tactic use on itself a carried thing whose `use` trigger mends once the mind is down to `mend_at_pct` of its health, and otherwise point one whose use is aimed at foes (`Aim::Foe`) at a foe, or one that mends and is aimed at allies (`Aim::Ally`) at a hurt ally, as an ability of that aim would be pointed, by `Aim::worth_aiming_at` and the thing's own `Targeting`, on `chance_pct` of the turns there is somewhere worth pointing it.
  A thing aimed at anyone or at the ground is never pointed by a mind: the engine cannot tell whether one aimed at anyone helps whoever it lands on, as a wand that copies what it strikes is, and one aimed at the ground may carry its user there, as a blink does, which no tactic has weighed.
  A game puts `UseCarried` in a brain to have it, and a kind that should leave its bag alone leaves `"uses"` out of its wits.
  `ItemsPlugin` tells a mind with the wits what it may use as `Snapshot::carried`, a list of `Carried { item, targeting, mends }` in `rl_rules`, narrowed by `may_use` asked as one that knows what it carries, and never a thing whose use asks for another thing, since nothing chooses which for a mind; whether a use mends is the new `Effect::mends`, false unless an effect says so, as the engine's `Mend` does, and `Effects::mends` asks it of a whole list.
  What a mind chooses is `Decision::UseItem` or `Decision::UseItemAt`, written as the `UseItem` and `UseItemAt` intents a player's use is, so the items resolver refuses a mind whatever it would refuse the player, and the narrator tells it by the line it tells the player's own by: `The goblin uses a potion of healing.`
  `Unknown` is the player's not knowing and nobody else's: the items resolver aims and refuses anyone else's use of a thing not yet known by what the thing truly is, as for a thing known, where it used to spare every user.
  `UseAbility` no longer points an ability where its flight would stop short of the cell it was pointed at, which the resolver refuses, so a caster no longer spends a turn on it; `UseCarried` points by the same rule.
  To change: a `match` over `Decision` gains two arms, a `Snapshot` built field by field gains `carried`, and a content file that lists every wit, as Corsair's, Delve's, Foundry's and Undermere's do, gains `"uses"`.
- Ammunition is the engine's, lifted out of Foundry: `rl_bevy::Ammo` names the tag one shot spends from its wielder's bag, and the opt-in `AmmoPlugin` runs `spend_ammo` then `sync_ammo`, chained in the new `AmmoSet::Resolve`, to spend it and to dry or reload the weapon from the bag alone, fresh every pass, with no event of its own to fall behind.
  A weapon with nothing left has its attack put by as the engine's own `Stowed` under a `Dry` marker, the same holder a game's own reason to bench a weapon (Foundry's heat among them) now reuses rather than inventing a second one, so `Loadout` falls through to the next worn item with no knowledge of ammunition at all; `Loadout::dry` answers whether an item has gone dry, the same "empty, not gone" standing an empty charged thing has.
  `AmmoChanged` is written for every dry or reload transition, for a game that wants to say so, as Foundry's own `ammo::narrate_ammo` now does in its place.
  `rl-ui`'s gear view notes a worn ammunition-fed weapon's count, or `dry`, as a facet in `ViewSet::Collect` rather than a game's own `ViewSet::Annotate`, since the engine knows ammunition now; a game with no ammunition-fed weapon adds nothing and pays nothing for the idea.
  Foundry moves onto it, keeping only its content and its own narration; its fingerprint does not move.
- A thing used from the pack can be aimed, as an ability is: a `use` trigger may carry a `targeting`, written `targeting: (mode: Bolt(range: 7), aim: Ground, sight: false)`, the shape, what it wants under it and whether its cell must be seen, in the words an ability is written in, `mode`, `aim` and `sight`, and a foe in sight unless it says otherwise; `Targeting` in `rl_rules` is the type, and `AbilityDef::targeting` gives an ability's.
  A trigger refuses a word it does not read, as a targeting does, so a misspelt one fails the load rather than leaving a staff landing on whoever zapped it; a trigger file carrying a field the engine never read now names it.
  The bag's use key on such a thing writes `AimUse { user, item }`, which opens the targeting cursor as `AimThrow` does and previews through `Bystanders::land`; confirming writes `UseItemAt { item, at }`, `UseItem`'s sibling, and putting the cursor away spends nothing and passes no turn.
  The items resolver refuses an aim by the rule an ability's is refused by, so a use the cursor shows as legal is one it takes, and `land_triggers` lands the trigger on its footprint from where its user stands, with the thing's `EffectBonus`; with a `look` it is seen to fly there, and the turns wait while it does.
  A `UseItem` of a thing whose use is aimed and a `UseItemAt` of one whose use is not are refused for nothing, and `AimUse` of a thing whose use lands on its user uses it at once, so a game's own key may write `AimUse` for anything.
  `AimUse` of a thing that cannot be used now, an empty one its user knows or one worn to be used and not worn, opens no cursor, by `may_use`, the rule the items resolver refuses every use by, in `rl_bevy`, which takes whether the thing is `Unknown` as its last argument.
  A `targeting` is refused on any trigger but a `use`, aimed at nobody but its user, or beside a second `use` trigger, and an item file that lists every trigger field, as Foundry's, Corsair's and Undermere's do, gains a line for it.
  `Aimed` carries a `Source` and a `Targeting` in place of an ability and its definition, `Aimed::ability` builds one for an ability, `aim_blocked` takes a `Targeting`, a `Trigger` written out by hand says `targeting: None`, and an `ItemRow` built by hand says `targeting: None` too.
- A thing can be used on another of its user's things, be not yet known, or refuse to come off.
  A `use` trigger may ask for a thing, written `asks: (prompt: "Hone which?", tag: "edge")`, `Asks` in `rl_rules`, its tag resolved at load and any thing carried but the one used when it names none; the bag's use key then turns the bag to choosing, titled with the prompt and listing only what the use may be made on, confirm writes `UseOn { item, on }`, `UseItem`'s second sibling, and putting the bag away spends nothing and passes no turn.
  The items resolver refuses a choice by `may_use_on` in `rl_bevy`, the rule the bag offers its choices by, and reports the use as the `use` moment where its user stands beside a new `FiredOn` message naming the thing, which `land_triggers` hands to the trigger's effects as `Landing::on`; the engine's own effects never read it, and a game's own effect that makes a thing better or known does.
  A `UseItem` of a thing whose use asks is refused for nothing, as is a `UseOn` of one whose use does not, and `AimUse` of one opens the bag choosing for it rather than the cursor, so a game's own use key still writes `AimUse` for anything.
  An `asks` is refused on any trigger but a `use`, beside a `targeting`, or beside a second `use` trigger, and an item file that lists every trigger field, as Foundry's, Corsair's and Undermere's do, gains a line for it.
  `Unknown` on an item keeps what it does and what it is worth off the bag, the look cursor's panel and the targeting box, which show its name and where it goes and say "unknown" in their place, while its real triggers still work; `ItemRow::unknown`, `InspectView::unknown` and `TargetView::unknown` say so, the box leaves out an unknown thing's reach, and the gear rail its charges.
  A thing not yet known is used, aimed and refused by nothing only its kind, its reach or its charges would explain, since a refusal for nothing tells as much as a panel: an empty one keeps its use key, `ItemRow::empty` is never set for it, and its use spends the turn and lands nothing, `land_triggers` landing no `use` of a thing with nothing left; its aim is previewed as a plain line to the cursor and the cursor's cell, with no footprint, reach, refusal or target, and cycles as `Aim::Anyone` would; and `UseItemAt` refuses it for no reach, sight or shape, making it where it may still be pointed, by `rl_rules::ability::made_at`, so it lands by its true targeting where its flight stops, at its reach along the line and on nobody its aim does not want. A known thing is previewed and refused as before.
  `TargetView::facets` is what a game adds about an aim in `ViewSet::Annotate`, printed by the targeting box under the chance to hit, as `InspectView::facets` is under the look cursor's forecast.
  A thrown thing not yet known is previewed as any throw is, its flight and the cell it comes down on and never its landing's area, but gives no chance to hit in the targeting box, since only a thing that strikes is rolled and its bag row already hides what it strikes for.
  `Stuck` on an item keeps it on while it is worn: taking it off, dropping or throwing it, and putting on anything that would push it off are each refused as any impossible action is, the player keeping the turn, and reported as `ItemEvent::WouldNotComeOff`, which the narrator says as "A ring will not come off." through the new `Phrase::WillNotComeOff`; the bag offers no key that would take it off and says it will not come off, by `ItemRow::stuck`, and a mind does not reach for gear that would push it off.
  The rule is `Equipment::equip_keeping` in `rl_rules`, where a thing that may go in more than one slot takes the one whose occupant may come off, and `EquipError` gains `Kept(slot)`.
  Neither marker is put on, taken off or saved by the engine: both are the game's, as what makes a thing known or frees it is.
  A `Trigger` written out by hand says `asks: None` and a `Landing` says `on: None`, a match on every `ItemEvent`, `EquipError` or `Phrase` gains an arm, and `ItemRow` gains `asks`, `unknown` and `stuck` and `InventoryMenu` gains `choosing`.
- The armor the vitals show is marked as not settled while anything the player wears is `Unknown`: `VitalsView::armor_uncertain`, and the vitals panel writes it `armor 5?`, since the total a blow meets still counts what the unknown thing lends, which a game writes from its level.
  The collector counts the true total, and the look cursor's forecast the true numbers of both sides, since only a game knows what a thing not yet known seems to be; a game whose true total would tell a level writes `VitalsView::armor`, `InspectView::duel` and `odds`, and `TargetView::odds` again in `ViewSet::Annotate`, as Undermere does with each unknown thing at its kind's plain numbers, saying so under the forecast and the targeting box's chance in a facet.
- `noun::plural` counts the word before any of a short list of lower-case connectives, `titled`, `called`, `named`, `from`, `with` and a few more, as it counted the word before `of`, and keeps what follows as written: `2 scrolls titled XOMBA RENTOR`, where it said `scroll titled XOMBA RENTORs`, so a thing named that way can stack.
- The bag counts the rows it has no room for, `▲ 2 more` on the frame over its list and `▼ 3 more` on the rule under it, in the muted tone, and its list holds still under a cursor moving inside it, moving only as far as keeps the row picked out in view.
- Harm an ability or a thing landed is told by what landed it: `Reach` gains `Ability(AbilityId)` and `Thing(Entity)`, which the engine's `Harm` stamps from its landing through `Landing::reach`, a prop springing its own trap staying `Effect`, and the narrator says `Your ash rod hits the rat for 4.` or `The goblin's firebolt hits you for 3.` where it said `You hit the rat for 4.`, as for a blow.
  The words are six new phrases, `YourUseHits`, `UseHitsYou` and `OthersUseHits` and their forms for nothing, `YourUseHitsNothing`, `UseHitsYouForNothing` and `OthersUseHitsNothing`, so a match on every `Reach` gains the two new arms and a match on every `Phrase` the six.
- A status put on again while it is still on is the new `StatusEvent::Renewed`, its time refreshed or extended or another of it stacked, and `StatusEvent::Applied` is only a status that began, so the narrator tells a status once, when it begins, where a cloud keeping it up read `The rat is paralysed. (x3)`; a match on every `StatusEvent` gains the arm, as Corsair's own status lines did, skipping a renewed status rather than telling it again, since it was told when it began.
- A status can take its holder's turns: `StatusDef::holds`, written `holds: true` in a status file, and while one is on an actor `StatusPlugin` spends each of its turns as a wait before its mind or its player is asked, as work claims a worker's, so paralysis, a snare and sleep are each one line of a game's statuses.
  A held player's keys come to nothing and its turns pass without them, and a worker caught at its work loses those turns rather than working them.
  The hold ends when the status does, by its time or by a cure, and `Statuses::holding` says which status holds an actor, if any.
  A status file that lists every field in its header, as Corsair's does, gains a line for `holds`.
- A stat can make an actor faster or slower: `TurnRules::new().speed_stat(stat)`, in `rl_bevy` and the prelude, names a stat whose value is a percentage added to the hundred of every actor's own `Speed` wherever the turn loop reads it, so a hundred is twice as fast and minus fifty half as fast, and haste and slowness are statuses that put a modifier on it.
  The stat is one whose base is nought, and nothing takes an actor below a speed of one: an actor that is not to act at all is held by a status, not slowed to nothing.
  `Speeds` is the one reader, and `SheetView::speed` and `InspectView`'s duel forecast read it too, so neither disagrees with the turns about who is hasted.
  A game that names no stat changes nothing; Undermere names one, for haste, slowness and the cold.
- A blow or a shot may have a shape: `MeleeAttack::shaped` and `RangedAttack::shaped` take an `AttackShape`, in `rl_bevy` and the prelude and read from RON, `Sweep` for every foe beside the attacker and `Pierce(n)` for the target and the foes in the next `n` cells beyond it in line, short of a wall and, for a shot, of its range.
  Each foe struck is rolled for on its own and takes the weapon's `hit` triggers where it stands, and the whole is one swing, one `Struck` and one `fire` moment, so whatever counts heat or ammunition counts swings; a foe is whoever the attacker is hostile to, so an ally or a bystander beside a swing is passed over.
  A blow on a target that has not noticed its attacker lands harder: at `CombatRules::unaware(percent)` for every blow, or at the blow's own `MeleeAttack::unaware(percent)` in its place, read from stealth's `Aware` through the new `Watchers::unaware`; without `StealthPlugin` nobody is unaware, and a shot is never multiplied.
  A game that builds its attacks with `MeleeAttack::new` and `RangedAttack::new` and its rules with `CombatRules::new`, as every game here does, changes nothing.
  A game that writes one out field by field adds the new fields as `None`: `shape` on either attack, `unaware` on a `MeleeAttack`, and `unaware` on `CombatRules`.
- A game may ship its own typeface: `RoguelikePlugins::typeface` and `TerminalPlugin::typeface` take the bytes of a TrueType or OpenType file, as anything that becomes a `Cow<'static, [u8]>`, a slice compiled in with `include_bytes!` or a `Vec<u8>` read at run time with nothing leaked to keep it, and the terminal draws every glyph in it rather than in the system's monospace family, which is another face on every platform, lacks box rules and symbols on some, and is only ASCII in a browser.
  Bytes rather than an asset path: an asset arrives after the first frames are drawn, and on the web only from a page that serves the game's assets beside it.
  A game that gives none draws as before.
  `TerminalPlugin` is built with `TerminalPlugin::new(width, height, cell_size, font_size)`, as every other plugin with settings is, now that it has one a game may leave out; a game that wrote it out field by field writes that instead, and one on `RoguelikePlugins` changes nothing.
  Undermere is the worked example: it ships DejaVu Sans Mono beside its licence in `examples/undermere/assets/fonts/`, and its tests read the face's own character map to hold every glyph it draws, its content's, its title's and every screen's, to the face.
- `FarthestExit` now floods through a shut door as well as open floor, since a door can be passable without being walkable, where before it measured only what could be walked on now and so stopped at the first door, finding no farther than the room the start already stood in.
  The cell it picks is still always one to stand on, never the door itself, and where two cells are equally far it now always breaks the tie toward the lower one by `Point`'s own ordering rather than whichever an iterator happened to visit last.
  Heist's maps move: its way on used to average about eleven tiles from the way in and now averages past half the map's own span.
  Delve's move too, by a few tiles here and there, only from the tie-break, since its own doors are walkable and never met the bug.

## 0.3.0

A page for every system, a screen for the run's end, and a line drawn between an ability and a thing in the bag: one release, 2026-09-23.

- Lighting's own module doc had told a game to opt in by inserting a `Lighting` resource, which is how the subsystem worked before it was a plugin and is the reverse of the rule the engine states everywhere else: whether a system runs is decided by whether its plugin was added.
  `LightingPlugin` inserts `Lighting::dark()` itself, and a game that followed the doc and inserted the resource without adding the plugin got the worst pair available, a gate on every viewshed and no field cast to fill it, so the world went dark and nothing said why.

  Keeping station on allies is better for it, in the two ways the enemy-facing half was already better: a field descent that would widen the gap while closing is passed over, and giving way to a crowding ally never takes a step that brings it nearer, preferring one that keeps a clear line. Nothing on the enemy side moved, and Foundry's fingerprint is unchanged.
- Foundry opens on a title screen rather than dropping the player straight onto deck one: the works seen from the gantry, with the furnace breathing, sparks going up the chimney, chassis riding the assembly line and the welders firing on what passes under them, and two rows under it, drop in or walk away. Separately, `Said::seen` was answering two questions at once: it is true whenever the player is one of the two, which is right for "speak this at all" and wrong for "name the doer", so a droid firing from an unlit room read as "The line droid shoots you". `Said::who_seen` is the narrower fact, and `render` takes whether to honour it, because a game that turned `Phrasebook::speak_unseen` on asked for those lines named.
